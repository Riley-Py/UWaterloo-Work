- Loose, solid particles
	- Described as *unconsolidated* as sedimentary grains or particles are separate and unattached to one another
	- Rocks are described as *consolidated*, as they are a solid, coherent mass of material made up of smaller components
- Highly variable
- May contain individual, separated rocks, or rock fragments, or fossils (composed of minerals)
- Rocks may contain sediment that is attached together to create a rock
- Sediment can vary based on their formation, and is the basis for the origin of sedimentary rocks

### Three Sediment Types

- Diagram of where you can find them
	- ![[types-of-sediment.jpg]]

#### Chemical Sediment
- Formed from precipitation of minerals from a solution (sea water)
	- Example: Goderich and salt mines, where the salt is used to keep roads and walkways free of ice
#### Biogenic Sediment
- Formed as a result of biological processes
	- Can be composed of plants/animals or contain evidence of past life
	- Also called *bioclastic*, as it's similar to detrital sediment but has an organic origin
	- Also called *biochemical*, as it can be similar to chemical sediment and has an organic origin
- Produced in world's oceans from microorganisms

#### Detrital Sediment
- Formed from pre-existing material, composed of fragmented rock and mineral debris
	- Also known as *clastic* (individual grains or *clasts*)
	- *Detrital* describes the formation of this sediment
- Found all across Earth's surface

### Is Sediment Connected to Other Parts In The Rock Cycle?
- Connected to all parts of the rock cycle except to magma
	- Can be produced by all three rock types and is important in the formation of sedimentary rock
