
**1. Early Observations:**
- Ancient humans observed the **night sky with the naked eye**.
- Myths and stories: Constellations tied to **gods, goddesses, and legends**.
- Observations included **stars, planets, lunar cycles, and eclipses**.
**2. Astronomy → Science:**
- Astronomy is often called the **oldest science / mother of all sciences**.
- Early astronomy **laid the groundwork** for scientific thinking and the Industrial Revolution.
**3. Everyday Scientific Thinking:**
- Humans naturally **observe, hypothesize, and test**:
    - Example: Hitting a closed door → learning to open it first.
- **Systematic observation** distinguishes modern science from casual trial-and-error.
**4. Ancient Astronomy & Scientific Method:**
- Core activities that **used early scientific thinking**:
    - **Timekeeping** (daily cycles)
    - **Tracking seasons**
    - **Calendars**
    - **Monitoring lunar cycles**
    - **Observing planets and stars**
    - **Predicting eclipses**    
**5. Motivations:**
- Originally driven by:
    - **Religious festivals**
    - **Superstitions** (e.g., eclipses as ominous events)
    - **Astrology**
- Despite different motives, **prediction remained central**, a core aspect of science.
**6. Legacy:**
- Calendars and timekeeping methods **still influence modern life**.
- Applications like **farming cycles** remain relevant thousands of years later.

**Greek Contributions**
- Greek science influenced **Western astronomy**.
- Parallels existed in **other cultures**, but focus here is on Greeks.
- Knowledge preserved in **Library of Alexandria** (scrolls = books).
- Greeks were among the first to **create mathematical models of nature**.
- They sought **predictive explanations** without relying solely on myth or magic.
- **Earth-centered universe**: Earth at the center, planets on concentric spheres ("crystal balls").
- **Celestial sphere**: outermost layer with fixed stars.
- Planets move on **nested spheres**, allowing **prediction of positions**.
- Early example: model by **Eudoxus (~400 BC)**.
	- Complex but mathematically-based; symbolic use of “crystal” for transparent spheres.

**Eratosthenes** - **Measuring Earth's Radius (240 BC)**
- **Location:** Alexandria & Syene (ancient Egypt)
- **Distance between cities:** 5,000 stadia (~150 m per stadium → ~750 km)
- **Observation:** 
  - At noon on a certain day, **Syene**: Sun directly overhead (zenith)  
  - **Alexandria**: Sun 7° south of zenith
- **Assumption:** Earth is spherical
  - If flat, Sun would be overhead in both locations
  - 7° difference → curved surface
- Geometry: Distance / Full circle ratio
- Calculation: 7/360 x circumference of Earth = 5000 stadia
	- Solved: about 250 000 stadia
- Convert to km: ~42,000 km (modern value ~40,000 km) → **very accurate**
**Significance**
- Example of **scientific method**:
  1. Hypothesis: Earth is spherical
  2. Prediction: Sun not overhead everywhere
  3. Measurement: Angle of Sun in Alexandria
  4. Calculation: Circumference of Earth
- Entirely **mathematical**, no mythology involved
- Early demonstration of **quantitative science**

**Greek Astronomy – Planetary Motion & Ptolemy**
**Greek Geocentric Model**
- **Postulates:**
  1. Earth is the center of the universe
  2. Heavens are perfect → objects move in perfect circles
- **Scientific approach:** Started with assumptions, then tested predictions (sometimes wrong, e.g., geocentric)
**Retrograde Motion**
- Observed: Planets usually move **eastward**, sometimes **westward** (retrograde)
- Modern explanation:
  - Earth overtakes slower outer planets (Mars, Jupiter)
  - Apparent backward motion is due to relative motion of Earth and planet
  - Duration: ~10 weeks
**Ptolemy**
- Developed **sophisticated geocentric model**
- **Almagest:** Arabic translation preserved Greek knowledge
- **Epicycles:**
  - Planets move in small circles (*epicycles*) whose centers move around Earth
  - Forward/backward motion explained retrograde
  - Multiple epicycles (~5–6 per planet) needed for precise fit
- Accurate predictive model for >1500 years, despite wrong postulates
**Legacy**
- **Strengths:**
  - Mathematical, predictive, testable models
  - Correctly measured Earth's size, radius of Moon
- **Weakness:**
  - Planetary motion model based on incorrect assumption (Earth-centered)
- **Significance:** Root of modern science → predictive, mathematical, not myth-based

**Copernican Revolution – Heliocentric Breakthrough**
 **Copernicus (1473–1543)**
- **Key contribution:** Revived sun-centered (heliocentric) model, published 1543
- **Achievements:**
  - Placed Sun at center → Earth and planets orbit Sun
  - Explained **retrograde motion** naturally
  - Measured planetary distances in **astronomical units (AU)**
- **Limitations:**
  - Still assumed **perfect circular orbits**
  - Predictive accuracy not better than Ptolemy
**Tycho Brahe (1546–1601)**
- **Role:** Observer, not theorist
- **Contributions:**
  - Extremely precise measurements of planetary positions (pre-telescope)
  - Had royal support and large instruments
  - Could not detect stellar parallax → believed Earth still central
  - Proposed **Tychonic model:** Planets orbit Sun, Sun orbits Earth
  - Hired **Kepler** as assistant
**Johannes Kepler (1571–1630)**
- **Role:** Theoretical breakthrough
- **Achievements:**
  - Used Tycho’s precise data to analyze planetary motion
  - Attempted circular orbits → failed due to 8’ (arcminute) discrepancy
  - Discarded perfect-circle assumption → **introduced elliptical orbits**
- **Significance:**
  - 1 ellipse > many epicycles → simpler, more accurate model
  - Eliminated need for complex Greek/Ptolemaic epicycles
  - Key step in development of modern astronomy and physics

**Ellipses – Geometry & Properties**
**Definition**
- An **ellipse**: set of points where **sum of distances to two fixed points (foci)** is constant
- Drawing method:  
  - Tie a string in a loop around two pins (foci)
  - Keep string taut with a pencil → trace ellipse
**Axes**
- **Major axis:** longest diameter
- **Minor axis:** shortest diameter
- **Semi-major axis (a):** half of major axis
- **Semi-minor axis (b):** half of minor axis
**Note**
- Planets follow **elliptical orbits** with Sun at one focus (Kepler came up with this)
---
**Kepler’s Laws of Planetary Motion**
1. **First Law – Law of Ellipses**
- Planets move in **elliptical orbits** with the **Sun at one focus**.
- Closest point to Sun: **perihelion**  
- Farthest point from Sun: **aphelion**
 2. **Second Law – Law of Equal Areas**
- A line connecting **planet → Sun** sweeps out **equal areas in equal time**.
- Implication: **planets move faster when closer to Sun** and slower when farther away.
- Explains **variable orbital speed** along the ellipse.
 1. **Third Law – Law of Harmonies**
- Relationship between **orbital period (P)** and **semi-major axis (a)**:  
  $p^2 \propto a^3$ 
- In **astronomical units (AU) and years** ($p$ is orbital period; $a$ is average distance from sun in AU):  
  $P^2 = a^3$
- Farther planets → **longer orbital period**, slower average speed  
- Closer planets → **shorter orbital period**, faster average speed
 **Additional Notes**
- Laws **do not predict**:
  - Semi-major axis or orientation of orbits
  - Exact position of planets without measurement
- Kepler’s laws allow **prediction of motion** if orbit and orientation are known.
- Average orbital speed:  
  $v \approx \frac{2 \pi a}{P} \quad (\text{e.g., Earth ~30 km/s})$

 **Galileo and the Copernican Revolution**
**Copernican Objections & Galileo’s Responses**

| Objection | Galileo’s Contribution / Observation |
|-----------|------------------------------------|
| **Objects in air would be left behind if Earth moves** | Experiments show objects share Earth's motion. Example: tossing objects in moving vehicle – they land in your hand. Introduced concept: **inertia** – objects stay in motion unless acted on by a force. |
| **Heavens should be perfect (circular, unblemished)** | Observed **sunspots** on the Sun and **mountains/craters** on the Moon using a telescope → heavens are not perfect. |
| **No detectable stellar parallax → Earth cannot orbit Sun** | Telescope revealed the **Milky Way is made of countless stars**, implying stars are much farther away → explains why parallax is undetectable. |

**Key Observations**
- **Jupiter’s moons** (Galilean moons): orbit Jupiter, proving **not everything orbits Earth**.  
- **Phases of Venus**: full/crescent phases observed via telescope → contradict Ptolemaic model, supports Copernican heliocentric model.  
- **Transits of Venus**: observed like eclipses, further supports heliocentric system.

**Historical Notes**
- Galileo was forced by the **Catholic Church to recant (1633)**.  
- Book removed from Church index; ideas formally vindicated only in **1992**.  
- Giordano Bruno: other heliocentric advocates faced harsher consequences (burned at stake, 1600).

**Nature of Science**
 **Scientific Method Overview**
1. **Observation** → notice a phenomenon  
2. **Question** → ask why/how it occurs  
3. **Hypothesis** → propose a possible explanation  
4. **Prediction** → predict outcomes based on hypothesis  
5. **Test/Experiment** → check prediction against reality  
6. **Refine** → revise hypothesis if test fails; repeat cycle  
**Key points:**  
- More mature sciences have gone through many cycles.  
- Success = repeated accurate predictions.
**Hallmarks of Modern Science**
1. **Natural explanations only**: rely on observable, natural causes; no divine or mythical causes.  
2. **Occam’s Razor**: prefer the simplest explanation that works.  
3. **Testable predictions**: models must make predictions that can be falsified; failure leads to model revision or rejection.
**Scientific Theory**
- Explains **wide variety of observations** using **few principles**.  
- **More than a hypothesis**: has passed repeated tests and predictions.  
- Must have **large, compelling evidence**.  
- Can have minor failures, but critical or unfixable failures challenge the theory.  
- Everyday meanings of words (theory, law, etc.) differ from scientific meanings.
 **Example: Evolution**
- Darwin’s theory of evolution by natural selection is a **robust scientific theory**.  
-  It has successfully met every scientific test for over 100 years, but could be refined or challenged in the future.  
- **Key takeaway:** No scientific theory is ever absolutely certain; theories are always open to refinement.

**Astronomy vs. Astrology**
**Astronomy**
- **Science**: follows the scientific method.  
- **Focus**: understanding how stars, planets, and celestial objects work.  
- **Significance**: often called the "mother of all sciences" because it was one of the first fields to apply systematic scientific thinking.  
 **Astrology**
- **Not a science**: searches for hidden influences of celestial positions on human lives.  
- **Purpose**: predicting personal traits or events based on stars/planets.  
- **Validity**: tests show astrological predictions are no more accurate than chance; mainly entertainment.  
**Notes**
- Kepler, a founder of modern astronomy, earned money from astrology.  
- Astronomy and astrology share language/tools, but their goals and methods are fundamentally different.
