
**When Did Life Arise on Earth?**
- Life likely appeared **> 3.85 billion years ago**, shortly *after* the end of the heavy bombardment period.
- Early Earth was constantly hit by large meteors, making the surface extremely hostile.  
  Once the bombardment slowed, stable surfaces and oceans formed → conditions for life.
---
 **How Do We Know This?**
 **Fossils**
- Found in **sedimentary rock**, deposited layer by layer by rivers and oceans.
- Deeper layers = older.
- Some of the **oldest fossils** are microbe-like structures ~3.5 billion years old.
 **Carbon Isotopes**
- Certain life processes prefer particular isotopes (lighter carbon).
- Rocks older than 3.85 billion years contain isotope ratios strongly suggesting **biological activity**.
 **Radiometric Dating**
- Uses decay of unstable isotopes (like carbon-14, uranium, potassium) to determine absolute ages of rocks.
- This dating technique is what reveals the age structure of layered formations.
---
 **Geological Evidence: A Timeline in the Rocks**
 **Grand Canyon Example**
- Its exposed rock layers preserve **~2 billion years** of Earth’s geological history.
- Tectonic forces (Earth’s crust moving) lift and expose ancient layers.
- Similar fossil-rich formations exist in places like Dinosaur National Monument and Alberta (e.g., Badlands).
---
**What Did the Earliest Life Look Like?**
- **Not dinosaurs or plants** — those are *very* recent in Earth’s timeline.
- The earliest life forms were:
  - Bacteria-like microorganisms  
  - Found in rocks ~3.5 billion years old  
  - Confirmed by isotope evidence back to >3.85 billion years
Life was microbial for **over 3 billion years**.
---
**Geological Time Scales (Super Simplified)**
- **4.6 billion years ago** — Earth forms  
- **3.85+ billion years ago** — First microbial life appears  
- **3–2 billion years ago** — Microbes photosynthesize → **oxygen builds up**  
- **~540 million years ago** — Cambrian explosion (complex animals appear)  
- **~400 million years ago** — Life colonizes land  
- **~200 million years ago** — Dinosaurs  
- **~1 million years ago** — Early humans  
- **Today**
---
 **Why Is Oxygen Important?**
- Early Earth had almost **no atmospheric oxygen**.
- Oxygen was produced by **photosynthetic microbes** over billions of years.
- Once oxygen was abundant:
  - Complex cells (eukaryotes) flourished  
  - Multicellular life expanded  
  - Eventually: plants, animals, humans  
**Oxygen buildup is a major biosignature** astronomers look for on exoplanets today.
---
**Why This Matters for Life in the Universe**
- If life on Earth appeared shortly after bombardment ended, it might mean **life arises easily** when conditions are right.
- But complex life took billions of years → suggests **microbial life may be common**, complex life less so.
- Atmospheric signatures like oxygen or methane could help us detect life on exoplanets.
---
**Evolution Through Time**
- Life evolves through **Darwin's theory of natural selection**.
- All life on Earth likely shares a **common ancestor**, as shown by **DNA evidence**.
- The **fossil record** confirms that evolution occurs over time.  
 **Natural Selection Explained**
1. **Random mutations** occur in species over generations.
2. **Fitness determines survival**:
   - Organisms better adapted to their environment reproduce more.
   - Less fit organisms reproduce less or die off.
1. Over many generations, **beneficial traits accumulate**, leading to evolution.
**Key point:** Evolution is random in mutation, but natural selection is **non-random**, shaping which traits survive.
---
 **DNA Evidence and the Tree of Life**
- DNA allows scientists to **map genetic relationships** between species.
- The “Tree of Life” includes:
  - **Bacteria** and **Archaea** → earliest and simplest life forms
  - **Fungi, plants, and animals** → small branch of the tree
- All life shares some **common microbial ancestry**.
- Many ancient species went extinct; only some survive today.
---
 **Early Life on Earth**
- Earliest life may have resembled **bacteria living near deep-ocean hydrothermal vents**:
  - These environments are **low in oxygen** and very **hot**.
  - Life there **does not rely on sunlight**; bacteria gain energy from heat and chemicals from volcanic activity.
- Early Earth was **hot, heavily bombarded, and harsh**, similar to these deep-ocean conditions.
- These **resilient bacteria-like organisms** are our best guess for Earth’s first life forms.
---
**Timescales and Complexity**
- **Simple organisms** (microbes) dominated Earth for billions of years.
- **Complex organisms** (plants, animals, humans) arose much later, requiring:
  - Millions of generations
  - Significant evolutionary changes
- **Viruses and simple microbes evolve faster** due to rapid replication cycles.
---
**The Miller–Urey Experiment**  
**Goal:** Test whether the building blocks of life could form naturally on early Earth.
 **What They Did**
- Recreated early Earth conditions:
  - Hot, humid environment  
  - Water vapor  
  - Methane, ammonia, hydrogen  
  - Electrical sparks to simulate lightning
 **What They Found**
- **Simple organic molecules** formed spontaneously:
  - Amino acids (building blocks of proteins)
  - Other precursors to biological molecules  
- Not living cells — but the **raw ingredients of life**.
**Implication:**  
Chemical building blocks of life can arise **naturally** under the right conditions.

---
**From Chemistry → Biology**  
Life likely began through a long sequence of random reactions + natural selection at the molecular level.
 **Ingredients of Early Earth**
- Heavy meteor bombardment  
- Intense volcanic and tectonic activity  
- Hot oceans  
- Rich chemical environment  
 **Steps Scientists Think Happened**
1. **Random chemical reactions** formed complex molecules, including **RNA-like molecules**.
2. **RNA inside simple membranes** (lab-made “pre-cells” or protocells) formed naturally.
3. Some RNA molecules developed the ability to **self-replicate**.
4. **Molecular natural selection**:
   - RNA variants that copied themselves more effectively became more common.
5. Over time:
   - RNA-based systems → primitive cells  
   - Later, **DNA evolved from RNA**, giving more stable genetic storage.  
   - Eventually biological evolution took over, leading to all life today.
**Key idea:**  
Billions of years + random chemistry + natural selection → first cells.

---
 **Could Life Have Migrated to Earth? (Panspermia)**
**Planet-to-Planet Rock Exchange**  
- Venus, Earth, and Mars have all exchanged **huge amounts of rock**.  
- Big asteroid impacts can **eject chunks of crust** into space.
- We have meteorites from:
  - Mars on Earth  
  - The Moon on Earth  
 **Could Microbes Survive This?**
- Some bacteria can survive:
  - Extreme heat
  - Radiation
  - Freezing
  - Years in space  
**So It's Possible That:**
- Life started on Mars → was carried to Earth  
- Life started on Earth → traveled to Mars or Venus  
- Life originated elsewhere and arrived via asteroid fragments  
This idea is **plausible**, though **unproven**.
---
 **Formation of Oceans**
- **4.4 billion years ago:**  
  - Earth cools enough for water to condense.  
  - First oceans form from water in the atmosphere.
---
 **First Life and Oxygen**
- **~3.5 billion years ago:**  
  - Cyanobacteria emerge.  
  - Begin producing oxygen via photosynthesis.
- **~2 billion years ago:**  
  - Oxygen starts to build up in the atmosphere ("Great Oxidation Event").
---
 **Complex Life**
- **~500 million years ago:**  
  - Cambrian Explosion: rapid diversification of species.  
  - Life begins colonizing land.
---
 **Dinosaurs and Early Mammals**
- **~225–65 million years ago:**  
  - Dinosaurs dominate the land.  
  - Early mammals coexist with dinosaurs.  
- **~65 million years ago:**  
  - Mass extinction event (likely asteroid/comet impact).  
  - Dinosaurs go extinct; mammals survive and diversify.
---
 **Rise of Humans**
- **A few million years ago:**  
  - Hominoids, ancestors of humans, appear.  
---
 **Energy Source**
- Life needs energy to function.  
- Possible energy sources include:  
  - **Sunlight** → photosynthesis  
  - **Chemical reactions** → chemosynthesis  
  - **Internal heat** → geothermal energy  
- Energy alone is **not enough**; it drives processes but doesn't provide material for growth.
---
 **Nutrients / Building Materials**
- Life needs matter to build cells, tissues, and offspring.  
- Nutrients provide:  
  - Carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen, and other elements  
  - Molecules like proteins, nucleic acids, and lipids  
- Energy and nutrients together allow organisms to **grow, replicate, and survive**.
---
 **Liquid Water**
- All known life on Earth depends on liquid water.  
- Water is a **universal solvent** for chemical reactions in organisms.  
- It’s unclear if life could exist based on other liquids, but water is our first guess when searching elsewhere.
---
 **Basic Requirements for Life**
- **Energy source** → sunlight, chemical reactions, internal heat  
- **Nutrients** → building blocks for organisms and reproduction  
- **Liquid water** → necessary for chemical reactions in all known life  

Among these, **liquid water** is the most restrictive factor when searching for habitable planets.

---
 **Requirements for Surface Life**
- **Star System Age** → The star should be old enough to allow billions of years for evolution.  
  - High-mass stars die quickly and may not provide enough time.  
- **Stable Orbits** → Planets should have stable orbits to avoid extreme orbital variations.  
  - Binary or highly eccentric star systems may disrupt habitability.  
- **Habitable Zone (HZ)** → The region where liquid water can exist on the surface.  
  - Too close → water evaporates  
  - Too far → water freezes  
  - Example: In our solar system, Earth and Mars lie within the HZ.  
  - Smaller stars → HZ is closer in, narrower range due to lower luminosity.  
---
 **Additional Factors Supporting Habitability**
- **Volcanism** → Outgassing replenishes atmosphere and oceans  
- **Plate Tectonics** → Maintains carbon dioxide cycle and climate stability  
- **Magnetic Field** → Protects atmosphere from solar wind and radiation  

These factors helped make Earth habitable, but it’s unclear how necessary they are for other planets.

---
**Mars**
- **Water Evidence:**  
  - Dry riverbeds and channels indicate past flowing water.  
  - Subsurface ice and briny water exist today.  
- **Exploration:**  
  - Curiosity rover (2012) and other missions search for signs of habitability.  
  - Ingenuity helicopter demonstrates new exploration techniques.  
- **Current Status:**  
  - No direct evidence of life yet.  
  - Mars remains a top candidate for potential microbial life.

---
**Jovian Moons**
- **Europa, Ganymede, Callisto:**  
  - Rocky, roughly Earth-sized moons.  
  - Evidence for **subsurface oceans** beneath icy crusts.  
  - Potential energy sources are limited but might support microbial life.  
- **Io:**  
  - Too volcanic and hot for life.  

---
 **Saturnian Moons**
- **Titan:**  
  - Very cold; liquid water unlikely on the surface.  
  - Lakes of liquid methane and ethane exist.  
  - Organic compounds may form dunes of hydrocarbon sediments.  
  - Possible subsurface oceans from internal heat.  
- **Enceladus:**  
  - Ice fountains indicate **subsurface oceans**.  
  - Internal heat may maintain liquid water beneath icy surface.

---
 **General Notes**
- **Subsurface Life:**  
  - Many Solar System bodies may harbor **liquid water underground**, thanks to internal heat.  
  - Heat sources include leftover formation heat, radioactive decay, and tidal forces.  
- **Challenges:**  
  - Limited energy sources in icy moons.  
  - Detecting microbial life remotely is difficult.

**Energy Received from the Star**
- Star luminosity: \( L \) (in watts, SI units)  
- Distance to the planet: \( d \) (meters, or in AU for convenience)  
- Planet cross-sectional area: $\pi R^2$ 
**Incident energy per second on the planet:**
$E_\text{in} = \frac{L}{4 \pi d^2} \cdot \pi R^2 \cdot (1 - p)$ 
Where:  
- \( p \) = planetary albedo (reflectivity, fraction of light reflected)  
- \( 1 - p \) = fraction of light absorbed  
---
 **Thermal Radiation from the Planet**
- Stefan-Boltzmann law: $F = \sigma T^4$ 
- Planet surface area: $4 \pi R^2$
**Emitted energy per second:**
$E_\text{out} = \sigma T^4 \cdot 4 \pi R^2$
Where:  
- \( T \) = planet’s surface temperature (Kelvin)  
- $\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8} \, \mathrm{W/m^2 \, K^4}$ 
---
 **Temperature Balance**
Assume **steady state**: energy absorbed = energy emitted

$\frac{L}{4 \pi d^2} \cdot \pi R^2 \cdot (1 - p) = \sigma T^4 \cdot 4 \pi R^2$

- Planet radius cancels: \( R^2 \) appears on both sides  
- Solve for \( T \):
$T = \left( \frac{L (1 - p)}{16 \pi \sigma d^2} \right)^{1/4}$
---
 **Convenient Units (Solar System)**
- Distance \( d \) in AU  
- Luminosity relative to Sun: $L / L_\odot$
- Approximate formula:
$T \approx 280\,\mathrm{K} \times \left[ \frac{L / L_\odot}{d^2} (1 - p) \right]^{1/4}$
- Example: Earth  
  - $L / L_\odot = 1$
  - $d = 1 \, \mathrm{AU}$  
  - $p \approx 0.3$ 
  - $T \approx 288\,\mathrm{K}$ (close to actual Earth temperature)

---
 **Determining the Habitable Zone**
- **Condition for liquid water:**  
$273 \, \mathrm{K} \leq T \leq 373 \, \mathrm{K}$
- Solve for \( d \) to find the range of distances for habitability:

$d_\text{min} = \sqrt{\frac{L (1-p)}{16 \pi \sigma (373\,\mathrm{K})^4}}, \quad$
$d_\text{max} = \sqrt{\frac{L (1-p)}{16 \pi \sigma (273\,\mathrm{K})^4}}$
- **Closer to star** → water evaporates  
- **Farther from star** → water freezes  
Note: Reflectivity (\( p \)) significantly affects the habitable zone boundaries.
---
**Types of Potentially Habitable Worlds**
- **Moons with habitable surfaces**  
  - Examples: Some moons of Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus.  
  - Could have conditions similar to Earth’s surface.
- **Super-Earths & Water Worlds**  
  - Larger than Earth, may not have a solid surface.  
  - Life could exist entirely in oceans.
- **Subsurface Water Worlds**  
  - Planets or moons with liquid water beneath the surface.  
- **Orphan Planets**  
  - Planets not orbiting a star, freely floating in space.  
  - Detection is difficult; habitability is speculative.
Note: All of these possibilities are largely speculative.
---
**Detecting Life on Exoplanets**
**Spectral Signatures**
- Compare observed spectra of planets (e.g., Venus, Earth, Mars).  
- Focus on **infrared emission** and **absorption features**.
 **Key Absorption Features**
- **CO₂ (Carbon Dioxide):** Present on Venus, Earth, Mars.  
- **Sulfuric Acid:** Notable in Venus’ atmosphere.  
- **H₂O (Water Molecules):** Indicates presence of water.  
- **O₃ (Ozone):** Only detected on Earth.  
  - Ozone is linked to oxygen produced by life (plants, cyanobacteria).  
  - Presence of oxygen could be interpreted as a **potential biosignature**.

**The Drake Equation (Qualitative Form)**

An equation that estimates the number of civilizations we could communicate with.  
It’s less a true physics equation and more a way to organize our ignorance.
**N = N_HP × F_life × F_civ × F_now**
- **N_HP** — Total number of *habitable planets*  
  - Likely **billions** in the Milky Way.  
  - Once uncertain; now much better constrained after thousands of exoplanet detections.
- **F_life** — Fraction of habitable planets where life actually *appears*  
  - Totally unknown.
- **F_civ** — Fraction of life-bearing planets that develop *civilizations*  
  - Also unknown; on Earth it took ~4 billion years.
- **F_now** — Fraction of civilizations that exist *right now*  
  - A civilization that vanished 1 billion years ago doesn’t help us communicate today.  
  - Depends on how long civilizations survive.
---
**Civilization Lifetimes & Uncertainties**
- Human-level civilization has existed only for **~10,000 years**.  
- Unknown whether:
  - Civilizations tend to self-destruct  
  - OR they survive for millions of years  
- These uncertainties dominate the Drake Equation.
---
 **SETI: Searching for Extraterrestrial Intelligence**
 **Goal**  
Detect **deliberate signals** from extraterrestrial civilizations.
 **Why deliberate signals?**
- Artificial signals can be distinguished from natural radio emissions.  
- Assumes civilizations *want* to announce their presence.
**What SETI Does**
- Scans the sky for narrow-band, structured radio signals unlikely to occur naturally.  
- Looks for potential transmissions across different frequencies and regions of the sky.
---
 **Messages We’ve Sent**
- Humans have sent a few intentional signals.  
- Famous example: message beamed toward **globular cluster M13**.  
  - If a reply ever returns, it would take **42,000 years** total round-trip.
---
 **Interstellar Travel Challenges**
- **Current spacecraft speeds**:  
  - Voyager & Pioneer: ~40–50 km/s (~1/10,000 of the speed of light)  
  - Speed of light: 300,000 km/s  
- **Time to nearest stars**:  
  - At current speeds, ~10,000–100,000 years to reach the closest star.  
  - Generational travel might be necessary for realistic human exploration.  
- **Energy & hazards**:  
  - Enormous energy requirements.  
  - Interstellar particles become lethal cosmic rays at high speeds.  
  - Time dilation effects if approaching light speed.
---
 **Pioneer & Voyager Messages**
- **Pioneer Plaque**:  
  - Illustrates human figures and the solar system.  
  - Shows direction to nearby pulsars (as coordinates for Earth).  
  - Limitation: pulsars change over hundreds of thousands of years.  
- **Voyager Golden Record**:  
  - Similar purpose: communicate Earth’s existence and culture to extraterrestrials.  
---
**Fermi Paradox (“Where Are They?”)**
- Based on the Drake Equation and plausible numbers of civilizations:
  - If civilizations are common, the galaxy could be teeming with life.
  - Yet we haven’t detected any signals or visits.
**Possible Explanations:**
1. **We are alone**  
   - Life or intelligence may be extremely rare.  
   - Our civilization is uniquely precious.
1. **Civilizations exist, but interstellar travel is difficult**  
   - Takes hundreds of thousands of years, maybe multiple generations.  
   - Civilizations may not prioritize exploration.
1. **Civilizations are short-lived**  
   - Societal self-destruction limits contact and expansion.
1. **Galactic civilizations exist and we are just waiting to meet them**  
   - Could happen, but timing and distances make it unlikely.
---
 **Oumuamua: An Interstellar Visitor**
- **First detected interstellar object** (2017)  
  - Hyperbolic orbit → came from outside the solar system.  
  - Visited the inner solar system briefly before leaving.
- **Unique properties**:  
  - Unusually thin and elongated shape.  
  - Suggested by Avi Loeb: could be a **light sail** propelled by starlight.  
- **Significance**:  
  - First direct evidence of objects from outside our solar system.  
  - Opens discussions about interstellar artifacts and detection possibilities.
