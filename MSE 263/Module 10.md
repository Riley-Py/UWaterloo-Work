## Oligopoly
### Price Competition - Imperfect Substitutes
- Demand curve depends on prices set by other firms; consider firms that produce imperfect substitutes
- Conditions
	1. Each firm has demand function that shows how demand for product depends on their price and other firms' prices
	2. Reaction curve of firm's is its optimal price as a function of other firms' prices
	3. Firms' decisions are interrelated
		- *Nash equilibrium* - prices charged by all firms that each firm's price maximizes its profit given prices of other firms; solve as system of equations
			- Example
				- ![[Pasted image 20260730201704.png]]
- Example: $Q_1 = 48 - 3P_1 + 2P_2$ and ${MC} = 8$ 
	- Firm 1 should charge $P_1 = 12 + {1 \over 3}P_2$; this is the *reaction curve* of firm 1 of firm 2
	- Nash equilibrium occurs when $b_1(P_2) = b_2(P_1)$ 

### Price Competition with Perfect Substitutes - Quantity Competition
- Goods become better substitutes provides greater incentives to undercut price of competitor to attract market share; equilibrium prices are pushed down to marginal cost and firms earn zero profit
- Conditions
	1. Market has demand curve for same good; used to determine market price vs output; use inverse demand curve
	2. Firms' output are interrelated; Nash equilibrium again, but with quantity
- Same setup as the above, but now reaction curve is based around quantity, not price
