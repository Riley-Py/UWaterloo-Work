### Abstract
- Found that top and middle managers' shared emotions during smartphone innovation process caused cycles of behaviours that harmed both process/outcome
	- Top managers were afraid of external competitors/shareholders
	- Middle managers were afraid of internal groups (superiors, peers)
	- Top managers' fears led to exert pressure on middle managers and misinterpret communication by middle managers
	- Middle managers didn't share negative info with top managers, leading to top managers thinking that their tech capabilities were fine without investing into innovation
### Introduction
- Innovation requires information sharing between various groups within an organization
	- Example: Top managers are expected to translate themes to goals for middle management, and middle management is supposed to report on progress to get feedback
- Current channels of communication between top and middle management may not be good enough, as people may omit information in fear or refuse to listen to others
	- Middle managers' understandings transform to be different from top managers' perceptions
- Emotions influence people's choices/behaviours/social processes
	- Emotions are shared within groups as members share their emotions socially
- Organizations need specialization and integration
	- *Specialization* - no individual can handle everything
	- *Integration* - combine different viewpoints
	- As specialization increases, people in organization push issues up the chain, which slows down innovation
	- Integration decreases when communication channels are only for refinement of current products
### Innovation/Emotion
- *Emotion process* - someone who is exposed to a stimulus, registers it for meaning, and experiences feeling state changes, while exhibiting facial expressions/expressive cues
- Emotions accompany actions depending on the target of said emotion to said action
	- Example: fear can make people get a vaccine or not get one; the target of the fear varies
	- Emotions directed to specific actions reduces attention to other matters
	- Targets don't need to be real; they can be concepts like money/power
- Emotions can make people prefer small short-term benefits over longer-term benefits
