Ross and Simon both describe limitations in how people interpret and act, but at different stages.

Identify:
- One specific way attribution (Ross) and decision-making (Simon) reinforce each other
- One specific way they are incomplete or insufficient if used alone

## Response
One way attribution and decision-making reinforce each other are the way constraints are formed.  After the perception of a situation, then in Ross' theory, the observer is tasked at attributing, and eventually judging, the way the actor behaved in said situation.  In the attribution theory, it describes how one can predict an actor's actions through their past behaviours, which can give insight into their judgement.  Similarly,  in decision-making, after seeing the situation and attributing further action to prediction, according to Simon, this prediction can be made by seeing the constraints that one has in the situation, which are based on what an individual can control and what has to remain constant.
