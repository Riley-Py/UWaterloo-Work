## Question
Assess the claim: "Most bad decisions are actually the result of earlier failures in perception and attribution."

Your answer must:
- Identify where the claim is strongly supported by the readings
- Identify where it requires qualification or limitation

## Response
This claim is supported by the readings as people perceive the reason for bad decisions are based on the individual, but don't consider other factors like the organizational structure or the system in which the decision was made.  Dekker mentions how bad decisions are not an individual error, but a sign of trouble within a system itself.  As well, Simon complements this as decisions are the result of evaluating constraints within a system and figuring out what constraints can vary, and which constraints are constant within a system.  A bad decision could thus be created due to a system having constants which shouldn't be constant given a particular situation.  An example of this would be making the decision of buying a vehicle as a university student, losing their financial aid.  Taken on face value, we can say that the university student is not smart with money.  However, if we look deeper into the situation, we may see that this is a misattribution of the bad decision due to their trait, and not the situation.
