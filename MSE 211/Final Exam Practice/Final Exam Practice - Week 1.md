
## Question 1

Course introduction claims that many failures are predominately behavioural failures with technical symptoms.  Assess the claim.  Where does the evidence from this week's readings support it?  Where does it require qualification?

### Answer to Q1

Failures are predominately behavioural failures with technical symptoms as there may be procedures that aren't told to specific people as to how to react to a situation, which doesn't allow them to decide on an appropriate course of action.  This can result in failures within a system.  The readings this week support this through the Texas City Explosion, where workers weren't told by their managers on what procedures to follow, such as not knowing procedure to how to tell others when turnover happened within the oil refinery.  This enabled people who came on shift to not know what the others did before, which led to a lack awareness on potential issues that could lead to failure within a system, like alarms not triggering.  Additionally, within an organization, there may be negligence of necessary systems, such as safety systems, that would lead to failures.  In the Texas City Explosion example, upper-management, due to cost-cutting, decided against revamping active safety systems and instead focused on safety metrics, as BP would have had to shut down the plant if it didn't pass inspections by the OSHA.  Where the claim requires qualification is tracing specifically the pathway that led to the behavioural failure, like how it may have been due to how an organization interpreted the situation.
