### Answer to Q1

Organizations usually struggle more with implementing the right solutions, as it may take time and effort to put those solutions to action.  There may be many different levels of the organization that may have to travel through, and the solution may not be consistent with the culture that the organization has developed.  An example of this would be a family ran engineering organization, where the structure is bureaucratic and the top person is the one making the final decision.  All throughout those steps, that solution may be shot down and never reach the top person.  Thus, the claim that organizations usually struggle more with implementation than with identifying the technically correct answer has merit, but is lacking with regards to changing conditions within/outside the organization.  Implementing the solution takes time, but conditions both internal and externally of the organization could move rapidly in such a way that the solution isn't the correct one anymore.  This claim can be used to explain why some organizations seem on the surface to outsiders to not be doing the obvious, but not knowing that implementing the obvious takes much effort and may go against tradition.  As well, the claim can affect organizational performance, as if organizations can resolve implementation issues, then they can accelerate past the competition over organizations that can't implement fast enough/at all.
