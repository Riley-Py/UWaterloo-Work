
## Question 1

Assess the claim: 

"Organizations usually struggle more with implementation than with identifying the technically correct answer."

Your answer must:

- Identify where the claim is strongly supported (Pfeffer's knowing-versus-doing argument, organizational dependence, competing priorities, scarce resources, influence processes, implementation challenges)
- Identify where the claim requires qualification (technical uncertainty, poor analysis, insufficient expertise, inadequate information, design errors, changing conditions)
- Use at least one concrete example (engineering, healthcare, software, infrastructure, research, another technical environment)
- You must evaluate using explicit criteria, such as:
	- Explanatory power
	- Operational realism
	- Implementation effectiveness
	- Decision quality
	- Organizational performance

### Answer to Q1

Organizations do struggle with implementation, especially if they are trying to redo their overall structure of the organization.  An example of this would be a family ran engineering business.  There may be a person on top who is the CEO who, and then relatives who report directly to the CEO in a bureaucratic method.  As well, they may have traditions and norms that have developed over the years that are ingrained into the organization.  Thus, the claim that organizations usually struggle more with implementation than with identifying the technically correct answer has validity, but is lacking with regards to the changing conditions surrounding the organization.  The structure of the organization may change internally due to a shuffle in management, or laying off employees due to cost-cutting measures, or it may be externally driven, such as being bought out and absorbed into another organization.  This claim may be used to explain why some organizations are better with adapting to changes than others, even with identifying what the changes are.  As well, it can also be used to assess the decision quality that is being made, as the general structure of the organization influences how decisions are formed through perception and interpretation.
