### Answer to Q1

With behaviour, it can change depending on the situation in which someone is in that may not be align with personality.  An example of this would be in a robotics organization, when stress and pressure build up when meeting a timeline.  Someone may have a typical personality of being conscientious, but when stress is thrown into the mix, then their behaviour can display elements of neuroticism, which goes against their personality.  This is supported in the readings through Mischel's findings, where he stated that when environment isn't a constant, then so is behaviour, and that behaviour can't be predicted with an ever-changing environment.  In the example, no one would have predicted that this person would change from being organized to being anxious and stressed when stressors like timelines are thrown in.  Thus, the claim that personality is a poor explanation for behaviour in real-world settings may be true, but the limiting factor is that personality can be a predictor of behaviour if the environment is constant.  Going back to the example, say there was another person who was always agreeable; they worked well with others on the team and are generally well-liked.  If the environment doesn't change around them, then there is no reason for behaviour to change.  Thus, outsiders looking at the person would expect them to stay agreeable, and this would shape the way in which this person is delegated to roles.

Using concepts from both Week 1 and Week 2, explain full mechanism linking system conditions, decision process, behaviour

### Response to Q2 

The mechanism, starting from system conditions to the end behaviour, starts with the conditions of the work.  The project may be falling behind due to miscommunication within the group of individuals or due to management demanding features that take a considerable amount of time to implement.  This creates unsafe work conditions, as members are not being given the resources needed to implement the features.  This causes the individuals within the group to try and be more efficient, like skipping review steps within the project, to try and meet the demands of management.  However, the miscommunication between members within the group means that coordination to implement the features is not fully fledged, which the review steps would have caught in the process.  Thus, the individuals within the group act on impulse, and start to implement these features without communication with others and not reviewing if the features meet the specification, leading to the outcome of the submission having major errors.  These failures would be misinterpreted as individual failures as an outsider would only focus on individuals within the group; they would only focus on the outcome of the submission and blame it on the personality of the individuals, such as being lazy or not working hard enough.  However, if they took a step back and look at the entire situation, they would have seen that within the system, the working conditions were unsafe due to the strict time constraints without consideration for the amount of time needed to implement the features, leading to the members skipping the necessary steps required to make a robust submission.
