### Answer to Question 1

Engagement may be due to autonomy of an individual, as they are given the freedom to interpret the work as they see fit.  An example of this would be at a workplace where an employee is allowed to choose what work they would like to do from a job board.  They aren't micro-managed like in other workplaces where they are told what to do, which allows them to take ownership of the work, something that increases engagement and decreases turnover in studies about the workplace.   As well, an employee feeling competent about the work they are doing will feel more engaged with said work; if they are doing work that is within their realm of expertise, then they are more likely to get it done.  In the example, this person is able to choose jobs that match their expertise as a data analyst.  This is in opposition to doing work that doesn't match their competence, like sending basic emails and doing customer service.  Finally, feeling related to the work that is being done is important, as then one can align themselves with the goals of the organization at large.  With the example, this person relates to the importance of solid data analysis, and is thus more motivated to get the work done, as opposed to someone who doesn't connect with the goal, something that is related to the engagement-turnover relationship where there is more turnover the less engagement there is.  However, the claim that engagement isn't caused by individual motivation is missing a factor of interpretation variability.  Someone may interpret the goal of data analysis as valuable, while someone else may not interpret the goal as valuable, but still be motivated through extrinsic means like money to get the work done.
