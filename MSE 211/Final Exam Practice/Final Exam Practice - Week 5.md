### Answer to Question 1

Engagement may be due to autonomy of an individual, as they are given the freedom to interpret the work as they see fit.  An example of this would be at a workplace where an employee is allowed to choose what work they would like to do from a job board.  They aren't micro-managed like in other workplaces where they are told what to do, which allows them to take ownership of the work, something that increases engagement and decreases turnover in studies about the workplace.   As well, an employee feeling competent about the work they are doing will feel more engaged with said work; if they are doing work that is within their realm of expertise, then they are more likely to get it done.  In the example, this person is able to choose jobs that match their expertise as a data analyst.  This is in opposition to doing work that doesn't match their competence, like sending basic emails and doing customer service.  Finally, feeling related to the work that is being done is important, as then one can align themselves with the goals of the organization at large.  With the example, this person relates to the importance of solid data analysis, and is thus more motivated to get the work done, as opposed to someone who doesn't connect with the goal, something that is related to the engagement-turnover relationship where there is more turnover the less engagement there is.  However, the claim that engagement isn't caused by individual motivation is missing a factor of interpretation variability.  Someone may interpret the goal of data analysis as valuable, while someone else may not interpret the goal as valuable, but still be motivated through extrinsic means like money to get the work done.

### Answer to Q2

The full mechanism, from output initially increasing to quality decline, disengagement, and turnover, starts with the system conditions and how the work conditions were laid out.  From management, they laid out bonuses that were tied directly to speed.  Thus, the software engineering team started to make goals that surrounded that. Their goal was on how to get output out as quickly as possible in order to achieve those bonuses.  This makes the individuals on the team perceive that the work itself doesn't actually matter, but just the output - quality over quantity.  With this perception of quality over quantity, the software team interprets that management only cares for output without any regard to how they are feeling or what the end result is.  Since they know that management doesn't care about their work, the needs of the software team conflict.  On the one hand, they need the money in order to survive and meet their basic needs.  On the other, the needs of self-fulfilment, due to management not caring, aren't being met.  This leads to motivation that is extrinsic and fleeting, based around financial incentive.  However, once that motivation leaves, then since there is no intrinsic motivation due to not having any supports in place, the software engineering team decides that it isn't worth taking a quality over quantity approach and instead takes short cuts.  However, these software engineers take passion in their work, and they don't feel great about it.  With the weak motivation in play, they start to feel disengaged with the goal of meeting the output set out by management, and performance bonuses aren't enough of a motivator for them, leading to the turnovers. Behaviour initially improved as the team was driven extrinsically by finances.  However, studies have shown that extrinsic motivation isn't long lasting, and thus degraded over time as the engineer's other needs - the need of self-fulfilment - weren't being met.  This leads to why disengagement and turnover being predictable: when you start to lose sight of the goal and feel as though your abilities aren't being used to the maximum amount, you start to feel disengaged, and thus start looking for other opportunities that can utilize your abilities and you feel more related to the goal besides shallow extrinsic ones like money.  This outcome might be misinterpreted as individual failure, as on the surface, outsiders may see the engineers as lazy or greedy.  However, they don't see that within the environment they were working in, they didn't have long-term motivation.
