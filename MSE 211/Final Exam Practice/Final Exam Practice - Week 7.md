## Question 1

Week 7 challenges the assumption that groups improve decision quality automatically.

Assess the claim:

"Collective systems often distort information and evaluation in ways that make organizational decisions worse than individual decisions"

Your answer must:
- Identify where the claim is strongly supported (social loafing, diffusion of responsibility, distributed attention, groupthink)
- Identify where it requires qualification (role of expertise, benefits of coordination, collective problem-solving advantages)
- Use at least one concrete example (Nokia, Challenger, another organizational setting)

You must evaluate using explicit criteria, such as:
- Explanatory power
- Predictive usefulness
- Intervention value

### Answer to Question 1

Collective systems may distort information through the use everyone thinking alike without anyone speaking up in fear of not conforming to the group's culture and norms, something that is based in groupthink.  An example of this would be in the Challenger case, where there were problems that arose, but people didn't bring them up as they feared the reprimand from it.  If people were able to break away from groupthink, then many errors would be able to get caught and less distortion of information would happen.  In the Challenger scenario, if people spoke up about the issues with regards to the shuttle, then different decisions could have been made that would have allowed the shuttle mission to be successful.  Thus, the claim that collective systems distort information and evaluation in ways that make organizational decisions worse than individual decisions may be valid, but it comes at the limitation with not mentioning the role of expertise.  The claim doesn't fully explain how individual expertise can factor into the decision-making process.  In the Challenger example, there may have been someone who was an expert in a system whom, due to fear of reprimand, didn't speak up about issues present.  

## Question 2 - Bridge

An engineering organization is operating under competitive pressure.

Over time: 
- Different groups focus on different priorities
- Negative information is filtered upward
- Responsibility becomes unclear
- Dissent decreases
- Collective decisions become increasingly over-optimistic
- Performance degrades despite capable individuals

Explain the full mechanism using concepts from:
- Week 1 - system conditions and constraints
- Week 2 - decision-making under constraint
- Week 3 - perception and interpretation
- Week 4 - goal shaping attention
- Week 5 - motivation and pressure
- Week 6 - cognitive limits and stress
- Week 7 - group and organizational distortion mechanisms

Your answer must:
- Trace the full chain: system conditions->goals->perception->interpretation->distributed attention->communication distortion->evaluation distortion -> decision -> behaviour -> outcome
- Explain:
	- Why different groups developed different understandings
	- Why information became distorted
	- Why collective evaluation degraded 
	- Why accountability weakened 
	- Why the outcome was system-consistent rather than random

### Answer to Question 2

The full mechanism, starting from the organization to the undesirable outcome, starts with the system conditions with the working conditions that the organization is in.  There is much pressure that is happening within the organization to operate as efficiently as possible.  From this, the organization shapes their goals with the focus being on beating out the competition and making sure that they come out on top.  To meet this goal, the organization starts to perceive that they are always doing well, and with this, interpret any sort of information goes against this perception as not welcomed.  The organization thus starts to filter information to only what they want to hear, and only focus attention on the perception that they are the best and that no one else has any better ideas than them.  This starts to distort communication between groups within the organization, as groups are now focusing on the priority of meeting the goals of the organization to be the best, and so every time a dissent happens from some members of the organization,  they would get reprimanded for that, and thus dissent magically decreased from those members.  They decided that it wasn't worth trying to bring up the problems, and instead focused on only reporting the positives of the project, leading to the end performance degrading.  Different groups developed different understandings, as they are prioritized on meeting the goals of the organization to be as competitive as possible.  Some of them are focused on being competitive internally with development, while some of them are focused externally with the competition.  Information becomes distorted due to everyone trying to conform to the standards set out by management, which filters information to what they only want to hear.  This also plays into why evaluation degraded: since management only wanted to hear certain outcomes, evaluating the decisions started to feel worthless for the members of the organization.  Accountability for groups weakened as a result of the filtering of information, as each group was merely being overly-optimistic about everything, so if something went wrong, then they would ignore it.  The outcome was system-consistent, as the pressure made it so that the organization, in order to cope with it, decided to only get positive information instead of any potential negative information that may have aided in increasing performance through reevaluation and revision.
