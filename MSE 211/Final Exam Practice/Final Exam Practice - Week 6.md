## Question 1

Week 6 challenges the idea that poor decisions are primarily due to individual error.

Assess the claim: 

"Most engineering failures are caused by system conditions that overload human cognitive capacity, not by poor individual judgement."

Your answer must:

- Identify where the claim is strongly supported (working memory limits, cognitive load effects, stress research)
- Identify where it requires qualification (role of expertise, interpretation differences, variability in response)
- Use concrete example

You must evaluate using explicit criteria, such as:
- Explanatory power
- Predictive usefulness
- Intervention value

### Answer to Question 1

Overloading cognitive capacity can create failures due to the working conditions that one is in.  Someone may be in an environment where they are expected to output a lot of work without much aid or support.  An example of this would be in an engineering firm, where engineers are given jobs in order to fulfil.  They have to learn about these jobs - what the problems are, what are the possible solutions - within a small time frame.  This can create extraneous cognitive load, as the engineer is constantly distracted with other work that needs to get done while also doing the current job they were assigned to.  As well, this increases stress, which is shown to further increase cognitive load by worrying about the effects of being stressed, such as not getting enough sleep/movement.  This may be alleviated by altering the system conditions, where the jobs are more evenly distributed, The claim that most engineering failures that are caused by system conditions that overload human cognitive capacity and not individual judgement has some validity, but is limited in regards to how someone may interpret the causes of the engineering failures.  One may attribute it to the system conditions, but another may attribute it the individual and them not putting in their full effort.
