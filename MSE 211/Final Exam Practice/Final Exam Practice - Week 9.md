
## Question 1

Week 9 challenges the assumption that leadership effectiveness is primarily an individual characteristic.

Assess the claim: 

"Leadership effectiveness depends more on how leadership shapes communication, participation, and evaluation conditions than on leadership personality or style alone."

Your answer must:
- Identify where the claim is strongly supported (transformational leadership, authentic leadership, distributed leadership, complexity leadership, leadership conditioning concepts)
- Identify where the claim requires qualification (role of expertise, structural constraints, operational pressure, authority and accountability, coordination demands)
- Use at lest one concrete example (engineering organizations, healthcare, infrastructure systems, another organizational setting)

You must evaluate using explicit criteria, such as:
- Explanatory power
- Predictive usefulness

- Coordination implications

### Answer to Q1 

When leaders shape communication, participation, and evaluation conditions on the team, it points to the fact that the leader is wanting to elevate their subordinates to greater heights and make them believe in the future, transforming the followers from simply following orders to get rewarded to now truly believing in the goal set out.  An example of this would be in Apple, where Steve Jobs used to rouse employees with speeches, even if the employees didn't believe in themselves.  He made those employees believe that they can do anything through charisma and persuasion.  As well, leaders often allow for open communication and for true evaluation.  In the example, even though Steve Jobs sometimes made employees fear of giving bad news, he also didn't critique them when they did, and would often encourage it and let the person be rewarded.  Thus, the claim that leadership effectiveness depends more on how leadership shapes communication, participation, and evaluation conditions than on leadership personality or style is a starting point
