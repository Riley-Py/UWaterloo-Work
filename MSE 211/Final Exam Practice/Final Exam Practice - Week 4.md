### Answer to Question 1

Goal structure is different for each person.  Some people are motivated by extrinsic  factors, like money, and some are motivated by intrinsic values, like personal achievement.  Take for example the Wells Fargo scandal; employees were motivated to open more accounts in order to get bonuses tied to it.  This was an extrinsic motivation for the employees, as they wanted financial gain from it, and demonstrates how when a goal is set high, then people are more likely to go out and get it, something that is described within the goal-setting theory framework.  The claim that most performance differences are caused by differences in goal structure, not effort has merit, but also has limitations by itself without introducing anything else.  In the Wells Fargo scenario, employees thought of themselves as high-effort, signs of self-efficacy, which also led them to push hard to open as many accounts as possible to meet the quota for the bonuses attached to it.
