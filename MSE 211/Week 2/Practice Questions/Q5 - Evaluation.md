## Question
Assess the claim: 

"Personality is a poor primary explanation for behaviour in real-world settings"

## Response
This claim is strongly supported by the readings as personality can change based on the environment.  Mischel found in his research into behaviour that the personality, and therefore behaviour, can change if the environment has changed.  An example of this would be in a social situation.  If I am with friends, I am far more likely to be more outgoing and extroverted with them.  However, if the environment changed and suddenly I was in an unknown setting with no familiar faces, then I would become much more reserved and introverted, even though my personality is extroverted and outgoing.  The environment marked the transition from extroverted to somewhat neurotic.  Another way this claim is backed up is the fact that decision-making behaviour may go against personality due to the context of the situation.  Kahneman describes System 1 and System 2 thinking as System 1 being automatic, while System 2 being deliberate.  System 1 may go against someone's personality, which would be System 2 thinking, when the situation is changed.  An example of this would be deciding on what to sell/get rid of.  Typically, this person is very open, and as such, doesn't want to get rid of any possessions in fear of needing them again.  However, a move to a new place is coming up, and they are needing to pack up.  They are finding that there is too much to pack up, so they are deciding on what to get rid of and what to keep.  The way that they are getting rid of stuff is asking this question: "Does this spark joy?"  System 1 thinking tells us that this should be an automatic "Yes/No", while System 2 will try and rationalize, even though it's against System 1.  The limitation within this claim would be the fact that personality *can* be a great explanation for behaviour if, *and only if*, the environment hasn't changed at all where personality is typically exemplified.  An example of this would be in a job interview, where HR are asking behavioural questions that are similar to what you would be doing on the job.  The reason why they ask those is because past behaviour in a similar setting predicts future behaviour, and so the way someone responds to those questions can reveal much about one's personality, which would explain their future behaviour.
