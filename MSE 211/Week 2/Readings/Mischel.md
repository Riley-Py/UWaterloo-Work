## Consistency/Specificity
- Behaviour can be predicted when you take into consideration the situation where the behaviour was performed
- Examined which aspects of behaviour were consistent and which weren't in all situations
	- Intellect is consistent
	- Personality isn't consistent (i.e. attitudes)
- If environment hasn't changed, then past behaviour can be a predictor of current behaviour
	- If environment has changed, then the behaviour isn't predictable
	- People can also change their behaviour overtime as well
## Delayed Gratification
- Ability to delay gratification leads to development of self-control
	- Long-term goals require setting aside distractions
- Marshmallow experiment showed this
	- Some grabbed the marshmallow immediately, while others waited to get double the marshmallow
	- Tracked these children and found that the ones who waited were more self-assertive; were able to cope with roadblocks in life; and were more successful academically
- Another experiment like this was done; however, the difference was examining whether there was an effect of fathers being away from their children
	- Found that when fathers were away, children wanted immediate gratification
		- This may have been due to immaturity/psychological adjustment
- Another experiment like the first one was done; however, the difference was the children being exposed to a live model (adult choosing strategy), a symbolic model (description of adult choosing strategy), or no model
	- Found that live model was more effective than symbolic model, and that it influenced the behaviour of children dramatically
## Cognitive-Affective Processing System (CAPS)
- *Personality paradox* - "How do we reconcile our intuition and theories that personality is relatively stable with evidence that personality varies across different situations"
- Proposed dynamic personality system that takes into account these traits:
	1. Behavioural consistency accounting for specific scores on trait tests that indicate how individual is like in general 
	2. Consistency in how an individual varies across different situations
- This became known as CAPS
- Proved it with studying children in a summer camp
	- Identified "if..then..." situation behaviour that reflected unique and stable elements of each child's behaviour organization
	- Called *situation-behaviour profiles* for each child
	- Personality variables are called *cognitive-affective units (CAUs)*
		- Encodings, beliefs, affects, goals/values, self-regulatory plans
