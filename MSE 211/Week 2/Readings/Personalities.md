- There are five big factors when grouping personality traits
	1. *Extraversion* - talkative, energetic, assertive
	2. *Agreeableness* - sympathetic, kind, affectionate
	3. *Conscientiousness* - organized, thorough, planful
	4. *Neuroticism (Emotional Stability)* - tense, moody, anxious
	5. *Openness to Experience (Intellect, Imagination)* - wide interests, imaginative, insightful
- Exceptions are possible to these groupings, and traits within these groupings may not go together
	- Example: someone can be assertive, but not talkative
- *Big Five* is a coordinate system that maps which traits go together in people's descriptions/ratings of one another
	- Not a theory
	- Discovered through factor analysis
- *Five-Factor Model* is a model in the descriptive sense
	- Associated with studies of traits using personality questionnaires
	- Used interchangeably with Big Five as a term
- *Five-Factor Theory* is a grouping of propositions about nature/origin/development/relation of personality traits to other traits
	- Presents biological account of personality traits
