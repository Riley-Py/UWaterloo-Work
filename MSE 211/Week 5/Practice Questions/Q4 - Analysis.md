Self-Determination Theory provides a mechanism for motivation, but it depends on how system conditions are experienced.

Analyze: 
- One specific situation (student, workplace, team)

Identify:
- How the system appears to support motivation
- How it may actually undermine motivation through interpretation

## Response
An example of a system within a workplace that supports motivation would be at my current workplace, where my supervisor set up a "job board", where I get to pick and choose jobs that I would be interested in, and I would be able to work at them at my own pace.  This supports motivation, as one of the conditions for motivation to occur is for one to take ownership of the projects that they do and have control and say as to what projects they do.  It also aids that I get to choose tasks that I am the most competent on the job board itself.  This is what SDT advocates for the best possible outcome when it comes to achieving goals and being motivated to meet said goals - having the autonomy/ownership to get a job done, and have a choice in what job to do that best meets the individuals skillset.  However, the way that it might undermine motivation is through the fact that there may be too much choice; sometimes, I can get analysis-paralysis as to what task I should do out of the multitude of tasks, and so I may not be able to choose a task.  Additionally, the system itself doesn't have any due dates/deadlines, which is what SDT is critiqued for: autonomy leading to work not getting done due to being too laid-back and lassez-faire.  This may lead to blame being put on me, but if we look upwards towards the system put in place, the system actively encourages this behaviour, placing the decision-making and rate at which goals are achieved at me.
