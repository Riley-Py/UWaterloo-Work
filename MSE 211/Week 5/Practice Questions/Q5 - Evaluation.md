Assess the claim: 

"Most engagement differences are caused by system conditions affecting autonomy, competence, and relatedness, not by individual motivation."

## Response
The claim is true in the sense that conditions related to the system affect autonomy, competence, and relatedness and not by individual motivation by providing those aspects to people working.  Someone who doesn't have those aspects may not be fully engaged, even if motivated. An example of this would be within the Gandhi and Robison article, where they described that a workplace that has those three aspects have employees who are intrinsically motivated to work, while they found that workplaces that don't have that have employees who are more likely to walk away for other opportunities or be more extrinsically motivated, which isn't good for engagement and retention within a company.  A personal example supporting this evidence is on my first co-op job, I wasn't fully engaged as the job that I was doing didn't give me the responsibility to do my own projects - all projects were given out and I was told to do them without my input.  Additionally, the job itself was boring and didn't engage any of my technical aspects, and finally, it wasn't related to what I eventually wanted to do.  Even though I was motivated to do it by the money, it was weak, and as such, I was looking for other jobs on the job as that was how much I wanted to go to another job.  However, with my other job, it gave me those three aspects, so even though the amount of money was still the same, I was more engaged within that line of work, which aligns with what SDT states.  Where this requires qualification would be in regard to the current state that people are in.  In my example, I had most of my needs met, so I wanted my self-actualization needs met.  However, someone else who may have needed that job to pay off bills and food and housing may have been engaged with the job as they know that their needs are being met.
