Assess the claim: 
"Most performance differences are caused by differences in goal structure, not effort"

## Response

This claim is strongly supported when it comes to how people set goals and are motivated to meet them in the first place.  Performance differences arise when there are, or lack thereof, building blocks towards the goal.  An example of this would be two people trying to reach the goal of attaining a job.  Let's say person A is the higher performer, and person B the lower performer.  Person A, in order to try to get the job, has certain checkpoints put in place to monitor progress -such as the amount of interviews they are getting with their resume, or actually seeking feedback for their resume in order to get those interviews.  Person B, on the other hand, doesn't do any of that, and instead blindly applies to jobs, doesn't get any, and then complains and wonders why they aren't getting any interviews/offers whilst not seeking feedback.  Person A is demonstrating what Locke & Latham described as essential towards setting effective goals: seeking feedback along the way to monitor progress and to change strategies if the feedback is going against what they are currently doing.  However, this claim is missing an essential piece, and that is self-efficacy.  In order for person A to be able to seek that feedback and respond to it in such a way as to progress towards their goals, they need to be able to have high self-efficacy - they have to believe in themselves that they can attain that job.  Person B, with the way they are approaching the situation, has low self-efficacy, and as a result, will have a far harder time trying to get the job.  This is also described by Locke & Latham as part of their goal theory, and this gets extended by the motivation hub concept.  Person A may also have personal goals attached to this goal, such as providing for family or growing oneself, while person B may only be motivated by getting the job just for the sake of getting it and nothing more.  Overall, the claim is mostly correct in the sense that goal structure does play a role into the process of performance differences, but is also missing nuance when considering other factors like the motivation hub or self-efficacy of the individual whose performance is getting measured.
