Assess the claim: 
"Most performance differences are caused by differences in goal structure, not effort"

## Response

This claim is strongly supported when it comes to how people set goals and are motivated to meet them in the first place.  Performance differences arise when there are, or lack thereof, building blocks towards the goal.  An example of this would be two people trying to reach the goal of attaining a job.  Let's say person A is the higher performer, and person B the lower performer.  Person A, in order to try to get the job, has certain checkpoints put in place to monitor progress -such as the amount of interviews they are getting with their resume, or actually seeking feedback for their resume in order to get those interviews.  Person B, on the other hand, doesn't do any of that, and instead blindly applies to jobs, doesn't get any, and then complains and wonders why they aren't getting any interviews/offers whilst not seeking feedback.  Person A is demonstrating what Locke & Latham described as essential towards setting effective goals: seeking feedback along the way to monitor progress and to change strategies if the feedback is going against what they are currently doing.
