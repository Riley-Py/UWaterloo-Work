Maslow, Herzberg, and Locke & Latham describe motivation at different levels.

Identify:
- One specific way Maslow or Herzberg helps explain behaviour
- One specific way they are incomplete without a mechanism (goal-setting)

## Response
Maslow explains behaviour through the fact that people prioritize meeting certain needs before other needs.  An example of this would be a person trying to get a job.  Before this person can do this, though, they first need to deal with the basic necessitities of life: they need to be well-fed and have a house.  Then, they need to have a solid base where they have a support system in-place and stability before focusing their energy into trying to get a job.  In this example, Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs can be applied, as before the person is motivated to get a job, they first have other needs that must be fulfilled before getting to that point of being motivated to get a job, like human needs.  Without having a solid foundation, the person wanting a job will not be able to fully focus on that goal with full attention.  However, Maslow doesn't explain the goal-setting process within this example of the person getting a job, as it only explains about the needs of the person.  This is where more information is needed, and Locke & Latham explain that the mechanism for goal-setting for individuals.  Using the example, in order for the person to try and get that job, this goal must be difficult enough to keep the person engaged, and there must be feedback for the progress of said goal for the person - such as the status of job applications, or asking what could be improved upon for job interviews on the journey to get the job.  Locke & Latham support this with their research with regards to the fact that for one to be motivated to achieve the goal that they set out, the goal must be difficult enough, and feedback must be given at thresholds in order for the person to keep striving for that goal.
