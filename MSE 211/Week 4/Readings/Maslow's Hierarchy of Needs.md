### Overview
- Motivational theory in psychology depicting needs
	- Represented in a pyramid
	- Lower levels must be satisfied before going upwards
- Diagram of this:
	 ![[Pasted image 20260603101007.png]]
- Expansions added cognitive, aesthetic, and transcendence needs
- People pursue multiple needs simultaneously, contradicting the original theory
- Different societies prioritize different needs
- Identify barriers to motivation and guides strategies to fulfill various needs
- Has garnered criticism due to biographical approach, cultural biases present, and debate on the ordering of needs makes the theory a *starting point*
### Explanation
- Maslow states that survival needs must be satisfied before higher needs
- Higher up the hierarchy, needs are more difficult to satisfy
	- Due to interpersonal/environmental barriers
- Higher up the hierarchy, needs become more psychological/long-term over physiological/short-term

#### Physiological Needs
- Needed for physical survival
- Human body can't function optimally if physiological needs aren't satisfied

#### Safety Needs
- Involves predictability, order, control
- Includes job security, health, financial security, freedom from harm
- Family and societal structures, such as law enforcement/medical care, can fulfill this
#### Love and Belonging Needs
- Social connection (friendship, family, romantic relationships)
- Strong in childhood and can override the need for safety
	- Witnessed in children clinging to abusive parents
#### Esteem Needs
- Self-respect, achievement, recognition
- Defined into two categories
	1. Esteem for oneself (dignity, independence, achievement)
	2. Respect from others (status, mastery, prestige)
- Inferiority complex may result from imbalances during this level
