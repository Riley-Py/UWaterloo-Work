## Question: 

Hackman's hospital study and Rasmussen's migration mechanism both explain how organizations produce bad outcomes through normal behaviour.  Identify one specific way the two explanations reinforce each other and one specific way they are in tension.

## Response:

A way that Hackman's hospital study and Rasmussen's migration mechanism both complement each other is the fact that even though they are in normal behaviour at the focal point, there may be variations within that differentiate them.  In Hackman's hospital study, for example, it was shown through bracketing that team dynamics at the lower level was what resulted in the variation between team errors where better managed teams had more errors than mis-manged teams. Similarly, in Rasmussen's migration mechanism, if the margin of normal operations is increased too much, then that can result in safety being deteriorated, which can then trickle down the organization silently and have levels blamed without a thorough analysis. The way that the two explanations are in contention is the fact that with Rasmussen's migration mechanism, the drive for efficiency in the face of safety may permeate across all levels or start at a level, then trickle down.  This is opposed to Hackman's hospital study, where bracketing found the variation in hospital rates down a level to the individual level, but not at the organizational level.  Rasmussen is looking at a focal situation from a systemic point of view, while Hackman is taking it levels at a time with bracketing, which can lead to either the entire structure being blamed, or a level being blamed.
