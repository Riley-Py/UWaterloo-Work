## Question:

The course introduction claims that many failures are predominantly behavioural failures with technical symptoms.  Assess this claim.  Where does the evidence from this week's readings support it strongly?  Where does it require qualification?

## Response:

The evidence from this week's readings support it through the Texas City Explosion.  The explosion, when viewed at a focal point, shows that the operators were the ones responsible for the explosion.  However, that would be faulty analysis, as when we apply Hackman's bracketing method to the focal situation - the explosion itself - it reveals that moving one level above to the organizational level, in the drive towards efficiency, there were cost-cutting measures that affected the training that the operators had and the equipment that was being utilized.  Moving one level below, it was also revealed that the supervisors of the operators didn't develop SOP documents to handle shift-handover, which resulted in miscommunication and eventually the accident.  Similarly, Rasmussen's migration boundary theory can be applied, where shortcuts and cuts in general throughout the entire organization made the margin of safety quite large and therefore, unsafe and risky.  This is similar to an event at high school, where people tried to get student dances free on the student council.  If we look at the situation face-first, it would seem as though student council was responsible for it.  However, if we bracket the situation and look one level above, we can see that the organization behind the student council, the teacher, has their own ideas and therefore prevented the change from happening.  This would be similar to the Texas City Explosion, where if we look at the situation, the operators would be to blamed.  However, if we look at it from a systemic point of view, then we can't necessarily put the blame on one party; rather, the culture and the drift from the safety boundary would be the reason why. 
The claim requires qualification as behavioural failures and technical symptoms can be intertwined.  An example from the Texas City Explosion readings would be how a chemist and an engineer created a chemical that personnel knew would react, but they still did it anyway without anyone protesting due to SOP.  This demonstrates how the technical symptom - the exploding chemical - and the behaviour failure - no one saying that there should be a review or someone to overlook that - can be together.  Going back to the student council example, the technical symptom - not being able to make dances free - is intertwined with the behavioural issue of not being allowed to by administration.
