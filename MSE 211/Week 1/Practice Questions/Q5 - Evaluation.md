## Question:

The course introduction claims that many failures are predominantly behavioural failures with technical symptoms.  Assess this claim.  Where does the evidence from this week's readings support it strongly?  Where does it require qualification?

## Response:

The evidence from this week's readings support it through the Texas City Explosion.  The explosion, when viewed at a focal point, shows that the operators were the ones responsible for the explosion.  However, that would be faulty analysis, as when we apply Hackman's bracketing method to the focal situation - the explosion itself - it reveals that moving one level above to the organizational level, in the drive towards efficiency, there were cost-cutting measures that affected the training that the operators had and the equipment that was being utilized.  Moving one level below, it was also revealed that the supervisors of the operators didn't develop SOP documents to handle shift-handover, which resulted in miscommunication and eventually the accident.  Similarly, Rasmussen's migration boundary theory can be applied, where shortcuts and cuts in general throughout the entire organization made the margin of safety quite large and therefore, unsafe and risky.
