- Culture is in between many disciplines, which allows it to be the centre point of said disciplines
- *Cultural island* - training setting is different from "back home" setting
- *System* - pattern of norms/attitudes across social unit
### Survey Research
- Property of groups that can be measured by questionnaires
- Problem is assuming what dimensions of culture should be studied

### Analytical Descriptive
- Viewed as concept where empirical measures can be developed
- Problem is that it compartmentalizes the concept of culture into smaller units instead of viewing culture holistically

### Ethnographic
- Concepts/methods from sociology/anthropology are applied
- Problem is that it's time consuming/expensive

### Historical 
- Looking to the past to see culture
- Problem is alike to ethnographic, but can be offset by insights that it provides

### Clinical Descriptive
- Consultants observe organizational phenomena as byproduct of their services for clients; data is gathered while consultant is actively helping the client system work on problems defined by client on their schedule
- Also known as *organization development*
- Problem is that it doesn't provide breadth like ethnography or rigour of hypothesis testing

## Definition of Organizational Culture
- Some organizations don't have culture due to frequent turnover/common history; others have strong culture due to shared history or shared intense experiences
- Group learns culture as it solves problems both internal and external
- There can be many subcultures within an organization
- *Culture* can be defined as such
	- Pattern of basic assumptions
	- Developed by given group
	- Learns to cope with adaptation/integration
	- Worked well enough to be valid
	- Taught to new members
	- Correct way to perceive, think, feel in relation to problems
- Aspects of culture are like defense mechanisms for individuals

## Levels of Culture
- Three levels of culture
	1. *Observable artifacts*
	2. *Values*
	3. *Basic underlying assumptions*

### Observable Artifacts
- *Artifacts* - physical layout, dress code, addressing people a certain way, smell/feel of place, emotional intensity, company records, products, philosophy, annual reports
- Problem is that they can be hard to decipher; can make incorrect inferences from them if not knowing how they connect to assumptions

### Values
- Can be found though surveys/interviews
- Includes norms, ideologies, charters, philosophies 
- How people feel/think

### Assumptions
- Determines perceptions, though processes, feelings, behaviour
- Once known, easier to decipher the aforementioned levels
