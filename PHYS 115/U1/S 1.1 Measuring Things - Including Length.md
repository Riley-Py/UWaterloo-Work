- **Unit**: Measure of a quantity (mass, time, length, pressure, etc.)
	- Attached to a **standard** - a physical quantity with 1.0 unit value 
		- Example: ${1.0 m} \over s$

## **Base Quantities & Standards**

- Independent quantities: Time, mass, weight, etc.
- Dependent quantities: Speed, velocity, etc.
- Seven fundamental quantities
	- Only three for mechanics: 
		- Length
		- Time
		- Mass
	- There are standards for each of them and define other physical quantities
- Base quantities are
	- Accessible
	- Invariable

## **International System of Units (SI)**

- Three base SI units
	- Meters (length) - m
	- Seconds (time) - s
	- Kilograms (mass) - kg
- Many derived units, written in terms of base units
	- Example #1: Joules (work - energy) - ${1 J} = {1 {kg}} \cdot {{m^2} \over {s^3}}$
	- Example #2: Watts (power) - ${1 W} = 1 {J \over s} = 1 {kg} \cdot {m^2 \over s^3}$

## **Scientific Notation/Conversion Factors**

- **Scientific notation** - powers of 10 to write numbers
	- Example #1:  ${3 560 000 000m} = 3.56 \times 10^{9}m$ 
	- Example #2: $0.000000492s = 4.92 \times 10 ^ {-7}s$
- **Conversion factor** - used to convert between units
	- Example: $2 {min} \times {60s \over 1 {min}} = 120s$
	- Units can cancel out and compound between each other
## **Accuracy & Reference Standards**
- 1 meter has been
	1. One ten-millionth of the distance from the North Pole to the equator
	2. A platinum-iridium standard meter bar
	3. 1 650 763.73 wave lengths of an emission line of Kr-86
- Nowadays, a meter is "length of the path traveled by light in a vacuum during a time interval of 1 / 299 792 458 of a second"
## **Significant Figures**

- Meaningful digits
	- Example: 187 has 3 sig figs
- Round to the *least number* of sig figs of the given data
- Sig figs *aren't* decimal places
	- Trailing zeroes are not siginificant
- **Note**: Just use scientific notation for all of this
