- These views are about how concepts are represented and structured

## The Classical View
- All examples or instances of a concept share characteristics (*features*)
- *Classical view of concepts* says that features are individually necessary and collectively sufficient
	- Individually necessary example: "has three sides" is a necessary feature of the concept of a triangle
	- Collectively sufficient example: "has three sides" and "closed, geometric shape" is sufficient to describe a triangle
- Assumes that concepts represent list of features; not representation of specific examples
- Assumes that membership in a category is clear; it either has the necessary features, or it doesn't
- Assumes that members within a category are created equal; no "better" or "worse" concept
