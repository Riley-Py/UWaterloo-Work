## The Formation of Other Solar Systems

### Revisiting the Nebular Theory
- Predicts that massive Jupiter-like planets should not form inside the frost line (at << 5 AU)
- Discovery of [[The Nature of Planets Around Other Stars|hot Jupiters]] has forced reexamination of the theory
- Planetary migration or gravitational encounters may explain hot Jupiters

### Planetary Migration
- Young planet's motion can create waves in a planet-forming disk
- Models show that matter in these waves can tug on planet, causing its orbit to migrate inward

### Gravitational Encounters + Resonances
- Close gravitational encounters between two massive planets can eject one planet while flinging the other into a highly elliptical orbit
	- One loses energy and the other gains energy due to the [[Physical Laws|conservation of energy]] 
- Multiple close encounters with smaller planetesimals can cause inward migration
- Resonances can also contribute to this

### Our System Rare?
- 20% of stars may have Earth-like planets in habitable zones

## Future of Extrasolar Planetary Science

### TESS & CHEOPS Missions
- Designed to monitor hundreds of thousands of the brightest and nearest stars across the whole sky for transit events
- Builds off of the Kepler mission

### Direct Detection
- Adaptive optics are aiding to enable direct planet detection
	- James Webb Space Telescope will aid in this
- Blocking the bright light from stars are also helping us to find planets around them
