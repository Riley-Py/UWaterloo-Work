## 1. All-Sky View

- **Observation:** Flattened all-sky image shows the **disk of the Milky Way** across the sky.  
- **Dark bands:** Caused by **dust clouds** absorbing light from background stars.  
- **High star density:** Visible along the disk.

---

## 2. Major Components

### (a) Bulge
- **Location:** Center of the galaxy.  
- **Shape:** Spherical region.  
- **Stellar motion:** Stars move in **random directions**.

### (b) Disk
- **Shape:** Flat, thin (~1,000 light-years thick, ~100,000 light-years diameter → 100:1 ratio).  
- **Sun's location:** ~27,000 light-years from the center (in the disk, closer to outer part).  
- **Contents:** Stars, gas, dust.  
  - Dust absorbs light → reddening of stars behind it.  
- **Spiral arms:** Non-uniform disk; Milky Way has **spiral arms**, some possibly with a bar.

### (c) Halo
- **Shape:** Spherical region surrounding the disk.  
- **Stars:** Sparse, moving in **random orbits**.

### (d) Globular Clusters
- **Location:** Mostly in the halo.  
- **Composition:** ~1 million stars each.  
- **Orbits:** Random, not bound to disk.
---
## 3Observational Notes

- **Edge-on view:** Disk and bulge prominent; halo and globular clusters harder to see.  
- **Angled view:** Shows spiral structure and possible bar.  
- **Gas and dust:** Cause **extinction and reddening**, requiring infrared observations to see stars behind dust.
---
**Summary**
- **Milky Way ≈ Typical Spiral Galaxy:** Disk, bulge, halo, globular clusters.  
- **Key features:** Thin disk, dense central bulge, sparse halo, spiral arms, and clusters.  
- **Stars and gas distributed non-uniformly**, spiral structure inferred from observations within the disk.
