
**Observing Galaxy Histories**
- We study **galaxy formation** by observing **distant galaxies**, which show us how galaxies looked **billions of years ago**.  
- **Look-back time** = time taken for light to reach us → higher look-back time = farther distance = earlier universe.  
- Observations:
  - Nearby galaxies: well-formed **spirals, ellipticals, irregulars**.
  - Early galaxies (high look-back time): **more irregular, smaller, chaotic**.
  - Mature galaxy types emerge over time; early galaxies lack obvious structure.
---
**Tools for Observing Early Galaxies**
- **Hubble Space Telescope**: provided extremely deep field images.  
- **James Webb Space Telescope**: successor, able to see **farther and earlier**  
- Observations guide **models of galaxy formation**.
---
##Models of Galaxy Formation
- Early universe: matter was **nearly uniform** (cosmic microwave background, 3 K).  
- **Gravity** amplifies small inhomogeneities → forms **protogalactic clouds**.  
- **Simulations** show:
  1. Uniform matter distribution → clumpy structures.
  2. Clumps merge → larger clumps → galaxies.

- **Protogalactic clouds**:
  - Mostly **hydrogen and helium gas**.
  - **Dark matter** also present, shapes gravitational potential.
  - First stars form in clouds → supernovae eject gas → limits star formation efficiency.

---

## 4. Disk Formation
- Gas that cools and conserves angular momentum settles into **disks**.  
- Disks are a common feature in **spiral galaxies**.  
- Angular momentum + cooling → organized structure.

---

## 5. Why Galaxies Differ
- Even with same basic physics, galaxies differ due to:
  - **Mergers and interactions**.
  - **Variations in initial conditions**.
  - **Environmental effects** (isolated vs. cluster galaxies).
- Examples:
  - **NGC 4414** → spiral galaxy.
  - **M87** → giant elliptical, hosts first imaged black hole.  
- Catalogs:
  - **NGC** = New General Catalog of non-stellar objects.
  - **Messier catalog** = earlier catalog of nebulae, star clusters, galaxies.

---

## Key Points
1. Early galaxies: **irregular, smaller, chaotic**.  
2. Mature galaxies: **spirals and ellipticals** emerge over billions of years.  
3. **Simulations + observations** show structure growth from uniform gas via gravity.  
4. Galaxy diversity arises from **mergers, environment, and angular momentum**.
