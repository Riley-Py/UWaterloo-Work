**Birthplaces of Stars**
- **Stars form in dense clouds of dusty gas in interstellar space**, known as **molecular clouds**.
- Evidence:  
  - Regions where stars are **blocked or obscured** by dust  
  - **Newborn stars** found within these dusty clouds
- **Interstellar Medium (ISM):** Gas and dust between stars
  - Gas: atoms and molecules  
  - Dust: large molecules or tiny solid particles (0.1–1 micron)
---
**Composition of Molecular Clouds**
- Determined via **absorption lines** in stellar spectra
- Typical composition in the Milky Way:
  - Hydrogen: ~70%  
  - Helium: ~28%  
  - Heavier elements (metals): ~2%
- Most common molecules: **H₂, CO**, and other simple molecules
- Conditions:
  - Temperature: 10–30 K  
  - Density: ~300 molecules/cm³
---
 **Observing Molecular Clouds**
**Optical (Visible Light)**
- Dense dust **blocks background light**  
- Example: **Horsehead Nebula**
- Observed as **dark patches** against brighter background
 **Infrared**
- **Longer wavelength light passes through dust**  
- Reveals **newborn stars** hidden in optical images  
- Used to study the **center of the Milky Way** and map stars orbiting the central black hole
**Radio / Sub-millimeter**
- Observes **molecular emission** (especially CO)  
- Dust grains absorb starlight and re-emit in **infrared/sub-millimeter**  
- Example: Dust at 30 K emits around **100 microns (0.1 mm)**
---
 **Dust in Molecular Clouds**
- Composed of **heavy elements**: C, O, Si, Fe  
- Sizes range from **hundreds of atoms to micron-sized particles**  
- Dust causes **interstellar reddening**:
  - Blue light is scattered/absorbed more than red light  
  - Stars viewed through dust appear **redder** (same principle as sunset color)
---
**Summary of Star Formation Environment**
- **Molecular clouds**: dense, cold regions of the ISM  
- Contain gas (H, He, metals) and dust (solid particles)  
- Stars form where **density is high enough for gravity to collapse gas**  
- Optical observations show **dark patches**, infrared/radio reveal **hidden stars and molecular emission**  
- Dust both **obscures light** and **re-emits absorbed energy** at longer wavelengths
---
**Gravity vs. Pressure**
- **Gravity** tries to collapse the cloud.  
- **Thermal pressure** resists collapse (pushes outward).  
- **Key idea:** A star forms only if **gravity overcomes pressure**.
**Role of Cooling**
- Molecules in the cloud emit **line emission** (infrared/radio photons).  
- This removes thermal energy → **reduces pressure** → gravity gradually dominates.  
---
**Conditions for Collapse**
- Typical **molecular cloud properties**:
  - Temperature: ~30 K  
  - Density: ~300 particles/cm³  
  - Mass: hundreds of solar masses
- **Critical mass (Jeans Mass):**  
  - Determines whether gravity can overcome pressure  
  - Example: 30 K, 300 particles/cm³ → **~170 solar masses**  
  - **If cloud < Jeans mass → pressure wins → no collapse**
  - $M_{balance} = 18 \cdot M_{sun} \sqrt({T^3 \over n})$ (Jean's mass formula)
	  - n is density per cubic centimeter
	  - T is temperatuer
---
**Additional Forces Opposing Gravity**
- **Magnetic fields**: create magnetic pressure  
- **Turbulence**: random bulk motions add pressure  
- **Combined effect:** These also need to be overcome for collapse
---
**Runaway Collapse**
- Once gravity dominates:
  - **Gas contracts → density increases → gravity stronger → collapse accelerates**
  - Thermal pressure may rise slightly but is offset by cooling
- **Outcome:** Collapse is unstable → runaway process
---
**Fragmentation and Star Clusters**
- Collapsing clouds **fragment into smaller pieces**  
- Each fragment can form a **single star**  
- **Large clouds** → hundreds/thousands of stars → **star clusters**  
- **Small clouds** → few stars (or even a single star)
**Turbulence and Clumping**
- Random motions in the cloud cause **lumps**  
- **Denser regions collapse first** → seed for individual stars
---
 **Summary**
1. **Gravity vs pressure:** star forms when gravity wins  
2. **Cooling:** emission lines reduce thermal pressure  
3. **Additional forces:** magnetic fields & turbulence resist collapse  
4. **Runaway collapse:** denser regions collapse faster  
5. **Fragmentation:** forms multiple stars → explains star clusters  
6. **Cloud properties (mass, density, temperature)** determine number and type of stars formed
