**1. Temperature and Density Evolution**
- Early universe: **extremely hot and dense**.
- Big Bang: universe at near-zero size, temperature ≈ 10³² K.
- Temperature decreases continuously over time:
  - 1 second after Big Bang: ~10¹⁰ K.
  - Present: ~3 K (cosmic microwave background radiation, CMB).
- Current age of the universe: ~14 billion years (~4 × 10¹⁷ s).
**2. Particle Creation and Annihilation**
- High-energy photons (gamma rays) can produce particle-antiparticle pairs:
  - Example: γ + γ → e⁻ + e⁺.
  - Threshold energy: 511 keV for electron-positron production.
- Inverse process also occurs: particle-antiparticle annihilation → gamma rays.
- Early universe filled with **particles and radiation in equilibrium** due to these conversions.
**3. Fundamental Forces in the Early Universe**
- Four forces: strong nuclear, electromagnetic, weak, and gravity.
- Force strengths vary with temperature:
  - Strong nuclear force: binds nuclei; weakens at higher energy.
  - Electromagnetism + weak force: unify at high energies (~10¹⁶–10¹⁷ K).
  - Gravity: weakest at particle scales, dominant on cosmic scales.
- Speculative high-energy unification:
  - **Grand Unified Theories (GUTs)**: strong, electromagnetic, and weak forces unify.
  - Even higher energies may unify with gravity (theories beyond Standard Model).
**4. Summary of Early Universe Features**
- Early universe extremely hot → high-energy particle production.
- Forces evolve with temperature → possible unification at extreme energies.
- Particle-radiation equilibrium dominated early universe dynamics.
- Provides the foundation for later processes: nucleosynthesis, structure formation, and cosmic microwave background.
