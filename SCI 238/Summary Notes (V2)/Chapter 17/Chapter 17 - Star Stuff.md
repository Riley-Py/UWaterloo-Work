## 1. Mass Determines Core Conditions
- **Core pressure & temperature** increase with stellar mass.
- **Higher mass → higher core temperature:**
  - Protons collide faster → fusion occurs more efficiently.
  - Luminosity increases (more energy output).
  - Faster fuel consumption → shorter stellar lifetime.
- **Lower mass → cooler core:**
  - Slower fusion.
  - Lower luminosity.
  - Very long lifetime (slow hydrogen burning).

---

**Mass Categories of Main Sequence Stars**
| Category | Mass Range | Approximate Lifetime | Notes |
|----------|------------|-------------------|-------|
| High-mass | > 8 M☉ | < 30 million years | Very luminous, fast fusion, short-lived |
| Intermediate | 2–8 M☉ | 100 million – 1 billion years | Moderate fusion rates and luminosity |
| Low-mass | < 2 M☉ | 1 billion – 10+ billion years | Slow fusion, low luminosity |
| Brown dwarfs | < 0.08 M☉ | – | “Failed stars,” core never hot enough for significant hydrogen fusion |

---
**Importance of Star Clusters**
- Clusters contain **stars of different masses formed at the same time**.
- Observing clusters allows:
  - Comparison of stellar evolution for different masses.
  - Verification of theoretical models.
  - Understanding of how mass affects luminosity, temperature, and lifetime.
- Clusters are crucial because they give a **snapshot of stars at the same age**, allowing for direct comparison across masses.
