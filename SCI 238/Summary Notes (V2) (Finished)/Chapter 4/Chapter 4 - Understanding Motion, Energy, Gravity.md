**Motion Basics**
 **Speed**
- **Definition**: rate of motion, scalar (no direction).  
- **Formula**: `speed = distance / time`  
- **Units**: m/s, km/h, mph.  
- **Example**: 10 m/s = 36 km/h.
**Velocity**
- **Definition**: vector; has **magnitude** (speed) and **direction**.  
- **Example**: 10 m/s due east vs. 10 m/s due west.  
- In space, velocity requires **3D specification** (two angles + speed).
**Acceleration**
- **Definition**: rate of change of velocity with time.  
- **Vector**: depends on changes in **magnitude** or **direction** of velocity.  
- **Examples**:  
  - Car turning while keeping speed → accelerating (direction change).  
  - Car slowing down → acceleration opposite to motion.  
  - Car speeding up → acceleration along direction of motion.
---
**Gravity and Free Fall**
**Acceleration due to Gravity (g)**
- All falling objects **accelerate at the same rate** (ignoring air resistance), independent of mass or composition.  
- **Newtonian gravity**: `g ≈ 10 m/s²` (varies slightly with location on Earth).  
- **Vector direction**: points **downward** toward Earth's center.  
**Velocity under gravity**
- Speed increases by **10 m/s every second** of free fall.  
  - 2 s → 20 m/s, 3 s → 30 m/s, etc.  
- Horizontal velocity is **unchanged** if gravity acts vertically.  
**Historical note**
- **Galileo** demonstrated that g is the same for all objects, disproving Aristotle's idea that heavier objects fall faster.

---
**Momentum and Angular Momentum**
**Linear Momentum**
- **Definition**: $( \mathbf{p} = m \mathbf{v} ) (mass × velocity)$.  
- **Vector quantity**: has both magnitude and direction.  
- **Newton's insight**:  
  - **Net force** changes momentum:  
    $\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}} = \frac{d\mathbf{p}}{dt}$
  - If mass is constant: net force → acceleration (change in velocity).  
  - For variable mass (e.g., rockets), both mass and velocity changes contribute.
 **Angular Momentum**
- Applies to **spinning or orbiting objects**: $( \mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{p} )$  
- **Conservation**: If no external torque → angular momentum remains constant.  
- **Torque**: Rate of change of angular momentum:  
  $\boldsymbol{\tau} = \frac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt}$
---
**Mass vs. Weight**
**Mass**
- **Definition**: Measure of the amount of matter in an object.  
- **Intrinsic property**: Does **not** change with location.  
- **Calculation**: Multiply mass of each component by the number of components (atoms/molecules).  
**Weight**
- **Definition**: Force exerted on an object by gravity or a scale.  
- **Depends on location**:  
  - Different on Earth at different locations (gravity varies slightly).  
  - **Weightless in freefall or space**: scale and object fall together → no net force.  
- **Formula**:  
	$W = m g$
where $g$ is local gravitational acceleration.
---
 **Weightlessness in Space**
- **Gravity exists in space**, but astronauts feel weightless due to **freefall**.  
- **Key idea**: Weight depends on a force pushing up (normal force).  
  - On Earth, scales push up → measure weight.  
  - In freefall, no force pushes up → apparent weight = 0.  
- **Orbiting objects**:  
  - Moving fast enough horizontally → fall around Earth instead of straight down → circular orbit.  
  - The spacecraft and astronaut accelerate at the same rate → no relative force → floating.  
- **Example**:  
  - Astronauts in a space station float because both the station and they are in freefall, moving along the same orbit.  
  - No upward force → no weight → apparent weightlessness.
---
**Motion, Momentum, and Angular Momentum**
**Linear Motion**
- **Speed**: Distance traveled per unit time.  
  - \( \text{speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}} \)  
  - Units: m/s, km/h, mph  
- **Velocity**: Speed **with direction** (vector).  
- **Acceleration**: Rate of change of velocity per unit time (vector).  
  - Can occur even if speed is constant (change in direction).  
**Gravity and Freefall**
- **Acceleration due to gravity**: $g \approx 10 \, \text{m/s}^2$ 
  - All objects fall at the same rate (ignoring air resistance).  
  - Speed increases by ~10 m/s every second of freefall.  
- **Weight vs Mass**:  
  - Mass: Amount of matter (intrinsic property).  
  - Weight: Force the scale exerts $W = mg$, depends on gravity.  
- **Weightlessness in space**:  
  - Freefall → no force pushing up → apparent weight = 0.  
  - Astronauts float because spacecraft and astronaut accelerate at same rate.  
  - Orbiting: horizontal motion + freefall → circular path around Earth.  
**Momentum**
- **Linear momentum**: $\vec{p} = m \vec{v}$  
  - Force changes momentum → acceleration.  
- **Angular momentum**: For objects spinning or moving in circles.  
  - Torque is needed to change angular momentum.  
  - Examples:  
    - Earth’s rotation around its axis → rotational angular momentum  
    - Earth orbiting Sun → orbital angular momentum  
  - Small torques from Moon/planets slowly change Earth’s rotation axis and orbit over long timescales.

 **Newton: The Scientist**
- Key figure after Kepler in the Scientific Revolution.
- Unified physics: same laws on Earth & in space → **one universe**.
- Major contributions:
  - **Laws of motion** & **gravity**
  - Early **optics** experiments, invented **reflecting telescope**
  - **Calculus** (co-invented with Leibniz)
- Most influential: **Newtonian mechanics**.
**Newton’s Laws of Motion**
**First Law – Law of Inertia**
- **Statement:** Object moves at constant velocity unless acted on by a **net force**.
- **Implication:** If no external force → velocity stays constant.
**Second Law – Force & Acceleration**
- **Form 1 (constant mass):**  
   $F = ma$
- **Form 2 (general/mass-changing):**  
  $F = \frac{dp}{dt} \quad \text{(force = rate of change of momentum)}$
- **Example – Rocket:**
  - Rocket ejects fuel → mass decreases → speed increases.
  - Momentum of rocket + ejecta remains constant if no external force.
**Third Law – Action & Reaction**
- **Statement:** For every force, there is an **equal and opposite** force.
- **Connection:** Can be interpreted as **momentum conservation** between two interacting objects.
- Works together with 1st & 2nd laws; sometimes laws are derivable from each other in certain limits.
 **Notes**
- Newton's laws are **interconnected**, but each has its own practical use.
- Momentum conservation = key idea linking **2nd & 3rd laws**.
---
**Conservation of Momentum**
**Why objects keep constant velocity (if no force)?**
- Because **momentum is conserved** when no external force acts.
**Example: Two Billiard Balls**
- Ball A (moving) hits Ball B (resting).
- If no external horizontal force:
  $p_{\text{total, before}} = p_{\text{total, after}}$
- Same masses:  
  - A stops → B moves with A’s original speed.
- Different masses:  
  - Heavy B → A bounces back; B moves slightly.
  - Momentum conservation still holds.
 **Key Idea**
- **Momentum conservation explains outcomes**, but sometimes must be combined with Newton’s laws for full details.
**Conservation of Angular Momentum**
**Definition (magnitude):**
$L = mvr$
- **v** = tangential (rotational) velocity  
- **r** = distance from center (e.g., from Sun)
**Why planets change speed in orbit?**
- Gravitational force is **radial**, so **no torque** → angular momentum constant.
- As r increases (aphelion), v decreases.  
- As r decreases (perihelion), v increases.  
- This keeps planets in stable orbits.
 **Kepler’s 2nd Law Connection**
- Areas swept per equal time intervals are equal.  
- Mathematically equivalent to **constant angular momentum**.
**Everyday Example: Figure Skater**
- Pulling arms inward decreases r → v increases (spins faster).  
- Same principle as planetary orbits.
 **When Do Conservation Laws Apply?**
- **Momentum conserved** when no external force.
- **Angular momentum conserved** when no external torque.
- Conservation laws simplify many problems, but **don’t give the full dynamics** like Newton’s laws do.
---
- **Energy is conserved**: total energy in an isolated system stays constant.
- Energy **transfers** between objects and **changes form**.
**Forms of Energy**
 **Kinetic Energy**
- Energy of motion.  
  Examples: moving cyclists, cars, orbiting planets (partly).
 **Radiative Energy**
- Energy carried by **light**.  
- Most energy on Earth ultimately comes from the **Sun**.
**Potential Energy**
- **Stored** energy; can convert to other forms.
- Examples: chemical energy in fuel; gravitational potential energy (GPE).
**Thermal Energy**
- Total **random kinetic energy** of particles (atoms/molecules).  
- Related to temperature but **not the same**:
  - **Temperature** = average kinetic energy per particle.  
  - **Thermal energy** = total kinetic energy of all particles  
    - depends on temperature **and** number of particles (density, volume).
**Nuclear / Mass Energy**
- Conversion of mass to energy:  
  $E = mc^2$
- Extremely efficient: small mass → huge energy.  
  Examples: fusion in the Sun, nuclear bombs.
 **Temperature Scales & Concepts**
 **Celsius (°C)**
- 0°C = water freezes  
- 100°C = water boils  
**Fahrenheit (°F)**
- 32°F freeze, 212°F boil
**Absolute Zero**
- Lowest possible temperature:  
  - **–273.15°C**, **0 K**, **≈ –460°F**
- Particles have *minimum* motion (never perfectly reachable).
**Kelvin (K)**
- Scientific scale; same size degree as Celsius.  
- 0 K = absolute zero  
- Water freezes at 273 K, boils at 373 K.
 **Gravitational Potential Energy (GPE)**
- $\text{GPE} = mgh$ 
- Depends on mass, gravity, and height.
- Example: water in a dam releases GPE → kinetic energy → electricity.
**Energy Conversion During a Fall**
- As an object falls:
  - GPE ↓  
  - KE ↑  
  - **Total energy constant**
**Astrophysical Example: Gas Cloud Collapse**
- Large cloud spread out → high gravitational energy.  
- As it contracts, GPE converts to **thermal energy**, heating the cloud.
 **Mass–Energy Conversion**
- Nuclear reactions convert mass into energy (fusion, fission).
 **Summary: Conservation of Energy**
- Energy **cannot be created or destroyed**.  
- It **moves** and **transforms**, but the **total stays constant** in an isolated system.

 **Newton’s Universal Law of Gravitation**
- Every mass **attracts** every other mass.
- Force magnitude:
  $F = G\,\frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$  
  - $(m_1, m_2)$ = masses  
  - $r$ = distance between centers  
  - $G = 6.7 \times 10^{-11}\,\text{N·m}^2/\text{kg}^2$
**Key Features**
- **Directly proportional** to product of masses.  
- **Inversely proportional to $r^2$** → farther apart → weaker force.  
- Newton’s 3rd law ensures forces are equal and opposite.  
- Applies to **everything**: planets, moons, bowling balls, stars, galaxies.
 **Gravity is weak (locally)**
- Gravity is the **weakest fundamental force**, but dominates on large scales because astronomical masses are huge.
---
 **Newton’s Gravity Extends Kepler’s Laws**
 **Kepler → Newton**
- Kepler described planetary motion.  
- Newton explained *why* and generalized it using:
  1. **Newton’s law of gravity** (force)
  2. **Newton’s 2nd law** (force → acceleration)
**Orbits from Newton’s Laws**
If only two objects interact gravitationally:
- Solutions are **conic sections**:
  - **Ellipses** → bound orbits (planets)
  - **Parabolas / Hyperbolas** → unbound orbits (comets, interstellar visitors)
- Kepler only observed ellipses; Newton predicted all conics.
---
**Center of Mass & Orbiting**
- Two objects orbit their **center of mass (CM)**.
- CM position determined by:
 $m_1 r_1 = m_2 r_2$
- Momentum conservation → CM does not accelerate without external force.
- For Sun–planet systems, CM is close to the Sun but **not exactly at its center**.

---
**Newton’s Version of Kepler’s Third Law**
For any two orbiting bodies:
- $P^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{G(m_1 + m_2)}\,a^3$
- $P$ = orbital period  
- $a$= semi-major axis  
- Works for **planets**, **moons**, **satellites**, **binary stars**, **exoplanets**, **stars orbiting black holes**, etc.
**Why It's Powerful**
- By measuring $P$ and $a$, you can find the **total mass** $m_1 + m_2$
- Examples:
  - Earth’s satellites → **mass of Earth**  
  - Jupiter’s moons → **mass of Jupiter**  
  - Stars orbiting the Milky Way’s center → **mass of the central black hole** (millions of solar masses)
---
 **Orbital Energy & Why Planets Speed Up/Slow Down**
- Total orbital energy is conserved when no external forces act:
- $E_{\text{total}} = K + U = \tfrac12 mv^2 - \frac{GMm}{r}$
- As an object **moves closer** to the central body → \(U\) becomes **more negative**, so **kinetic energy increases** → object speeds up.  
- As it moves **farther away** → \(U\) becomes **less negative**, so kinetic energy decreases → object slows down.  
- This matches the angular-momentum view (fast at perihelion, slow at aphelion).
**Changing an Orbit**
Energy can change **only through external forces**, such as:
- **Atmospheric drag** → satellites lose energy and eventually deorbit.  
- **Gravitational encounters** (“slingshots”) → comets or spacecraft can gain or lose orbital energy.  
- **Rocket propulsion** → engines do work → orbital energy increases.

---
 **Escape Velocity**
- An object escapes if its **kinetic energy is large enough** to overcome the entire gravitational potential well:
- $\tfrac12 mv^2 \ge \frac{GMm}{r}$
- Escape velocity from Earth’s surface:
- $v_{\text{esc}} \approx 11 \text{ km/s}$
- Smaller at higher altitude.  
- Reaching escape velocity means the total energy becomes **≥ 0**, so the object follows a **parabolic or hyperbolic (unbound)** trajectory.

---
**Tides: Gravity as a *Differential* Force**
Tides are caused not by the gravitational force itself, but by **differences** in the gravitational pull across Earth.
**Why?**
- Gravity follows an inverse-square law:
- $F \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$
- Side closer to the Moon feels **stronger** gravity than the far side.  
Relative to Earth’s center:
- Near side: pulled slightly more → bulge  
- Far side: pulled slightly less → opposite bulge  
This produces **two tidal bulges**: one facing the Moon and one opposite.
**Ocean vs. Land**
- On the open ocean the actual bulge height is only a few cm.  
- But when moving water is funneled into coastlines or bays, tides can reach **meters** (e.g., the Bay of Fundy).

---
**Why the Moon Dominates Earth’s Tides**
- Though the Sun exerts a far stronger gravitational force, **tides depend on force differences**, which scale as:
- $\text{tidal force} \propto \frac{1}{r^3}$ 
The Moon is **close enough** that its tidal effect is ≈ **3× stronger** than the Sun’s.
 **Spring and Neap Tides**
- **Spring tides (strongest)**: Sun and Moon aligned  
  - New moon or full moon  
- **Neap tides (weakest)**: Sun–Moon at right angles  
  - First quarter or third quarter

---

**Tidal Friction & Synchronous Rotation**
Earth’s rotation drags the tidal bulges slightly ahead of the Earth–Moon line, causing:
- **Loss of Earth’s rotational energy** (days get longer very slowly)  
- **Transfer of angular momentum to the Moon** → Moon slowly moves farther away  
- **Tidal locking of the Moon** → same side always faces Earth  
Tidal locking is common for close-orbiting bodies.

---
**Why All Objects Fall at the Same Rate**
Newton’s gravitational force:
$F = \frac{GMm}{r^2}$
Newton’s second law:
$F = ma$
Set them equal:
$ma = \frac{GMm}{r^2}$
The mass \(m\) of the falling object **cancels**, so:
$a = \frac{GM}{r^2}$
**Result**:
- Acceleration due to gravity depends **only on the mass of what’s pulling you (Earth)**.  
- Not on the mass or composition of the falling object.  
- This “universality of free fall” was discovered by **Galileo** and eventually became a key foundation for **general relativity**.
