- *Domain-specific problem solving* - only works with a limited set of problems

## Generate-and-Test Technique
- Consists of generating possible solutions and then testing them
- Loses effectiveness when there is no guidance for generation and when there are many possibilities, but useful if there aren't a lot of possibilities available

## Means-Ends Analysis
- Comparing the goal with the starting point, thinking of possible ways of overcoming the difference, and choosing the best one.
	- Selected option may have certain conditions in place before being able to execute the option
		- If those conditions aren't met, then *sub-goal* is created
- Forces the problem-solver to analyze aspects of the problem before starting to work on it and generate a plan to solve it
- Not optimal sometimes due to sometimes the most optimal way is taking a step backwards

## Working Backward
- Starts to the goal and works backwards to determine subgoals and conditions
- Famous example is the *Tower of Hanoi* problem
- Most effective when backward path is unique
- Shares with mean-ends analysis the technique of reducing differences between current state and goal state

- Using knowledge from one domain and applying it to another domain
