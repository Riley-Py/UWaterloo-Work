## **Dual-Task Performance**
- Spelke, Hirst, Neisser found that with practice, participants can perform two tasks at once with the same performance if they were doing one task at a time
	- ![[Pasted image 20250301231915.png]]
	- One hypothesis was that attention was alternating between tasks
	- Another hypothesis with evidence was the fact that with redundant information, the brain was filtering it out
- Another explanation was the fact that one of the tasks was being performed automatically
	- Posner and Snyder's criteria states that dictation might be considered automatic and they typically recognized about 80% of the dictated words on tests immediately following trials
	- Hirst found some evidence that this wasn't the case at all
- Hirst found another explanation, where participants learned to combine two separate tasks: reading & taking dictation
