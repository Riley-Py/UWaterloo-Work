- *Paradigm*: Knowledge that is structured in such a way such as the people who believe in it consider important and what isn't important

## **Information-Processing Approach**
- Draws an analogy between human cognition and computerized processing of information
	- Idea that cognition can be thought of as information (see, hear, read, think) passing through a system (humans)
- Assumes that information is processed in stages and is stored in specific places while being processed
- Also assumes that people's cognitive abilities can be thought of as "systems" of connected capacities
- Also assumes that people, like computers, are general-purpose symbol manipulators (can perform great cognitive feats by applying a few mental operations to symbols)

## **Connectionist Approach**
- *Connectionism (parallel-distributed processing (PDP))* - Models that show cognition as a network of connections among simple processing units
	- The models can also be called *neural networks*
	- Each unit is connected to other units in a large network and has some activation at any time
		- Activation depends on the input (environment/other units) to the unit and the connection between units have positive/negative weights
			- *Positive weight* - causes unit to raise the level of activation of other units connected to it
			- *Negative weight* - lowers the activation of connected units
