## Response

What we see from this unusual perspective of the Great Lakes taken from outer space is how this photograph, when interpreted through the perspective of Earth's internal systems, connects the hydrosphere to three other major Earth systems: the atmosphere, the biosphere, and the geosphere.  We see the sun reflecting off of the Great Lakes and clouds made of water covering them, reminding one of the greenhouse effect that the sun and water vapor contribute to in order to keep some energy in and to let most of it out to the solar system, regulating the temperature of the Earth and allowing living organisms to thrive on Earth.  As well, seeing the Great Lakes from this view makes one think of the freshwater that is essential to human life as humans need water everyday to survive, and how living organisms have come to rely upon water bodies like these to survive, allowing for civilization to continue through the ages and numerous species to propagate.  Finally, the way the Great Lakes are shaped and formed remind one of how water breaks down rocks and how it builds large reservoirs of water like the Great Lakes over thousands of years, shaping the Earth as we know it today.
